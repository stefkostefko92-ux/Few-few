Cookie banner blocker for Chrome

Supreme AdBlock answers cookie banners for you. It presses the banner's own Reject button when there is one, then clears the dimmed background and the scroll lock the banner leaves behind.

What happens when a banner appears

  1. It finds the banner's own refusal button, such as “Reject all”, “Necessary only” or “Decline”, and presses it, as you would.
  2. If there is no way to refuse, it presses Accept as a last resort, so you are not stuck behind the banner. Known ad and tracking requests stay blocked by the filter lists either way.
  3. It cleans up. A dimmed or blurred background, a locked scroll bar, a page that no longer takes clicks: all of it is cleared.
  4. It keeps count. The popup shows how many cookie banners it has rejected for you.

Works with the consent tools most sites use

Supreme AdBlock knows the refusal buttons of OneTrust, Cookiebot, Didomi, Quantcast, Usercentrics, TrustArc, Sourcepoint, iubenda, Complianz, Osano, CookieYes and consentmanager, and it finds the Reject button on many banners that sites build themselves. It also looks inside the frames and the shadow DOM that some consent tools draw their banners in.

What it never presses

A wrong click on a web page can do real harm, so the rules are strict:

  • Never a sign-in, OAuth or payment button.
  • Never a button that submits a form, and never a link that leaves the page.
  • A button found by its wording, such as “Reject” or “OK”, is pressed only inside a banner that is about cookies, never anywhere else on the page.
  • On sign-in and payment pages, it presses nothing found by wording at all.

Why it refuses first

In the EU, a site may use cookies that are not strictly necessary only with your consent, and refusing should be as easy as accepting. Some cookie tools make banners go away by accepting everything for you. Supreme AdBlock refuses first and accepts only when a banner leaves no other way out.

Your refusal is saved by the site's own consent tool, as if you had clicked it yourself, so the banner usually stays away on your next visit.

When you want to choose yourself

Switch off Cookie / consent banners in Settings and every banner is yours to answer again, which helps when you want a site to remember particular choices.

Switching it off does not let trackers in. Known ad and tracking requests are blocked by the filter lists, and third-party tracking cookies from known ad networks are stripped, whatever a banner says.

Questions about cookie banners

Is the cookie banner blocker a separate extension?

No. It is part of Supreme AdBlock, together with the ad and tracker blocking: one free extension, no account.

Does it reject cookies on every site?

On most. Banners change, and a new design can be missed. Report it from the popup with Site broken? so we can add it; until then, Hide element in the popup removes it from view.

Will rejecting cookies break websites?

Rarely. A refusal does not touch the cookies a site needs to work. If a page does misbehave, the popup can allow that site in one click.

Does it delete my cookies?

No. It answers banners and strips third-party tracking cookies from known ad networks. Cookies already in your browser stay until you clear them in the browser's settings.

Does it send anything about the sites I visit?

No. Everything happens on your device. The only thing it keeps is the number of banners rejected for you, with no list of sites.
