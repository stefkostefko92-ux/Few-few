Solutions · masonry or concrete lift shafts

Fixing guide rails and landing doors to uneven shaft walls

On irregular or out-of-square walls Panev brackets are adjusted on site: the articulated joint takes up the angle (± 7° / ± 8° for landing doors), the slotted holes let the fixing move, and the SG joining bracket slides on its support to cover the distance between wall and guide rail.

The problem on site

Along the lift shaft, cast-in-place concrete often has protrusions and recesses, and the wall is not always square to the component to be fixed. Traditional brackets do not adapt easily, forcing the installer into on-site modifications.

The Panev system is perforated, hinged and slotted: designed to adapt to irregular, out-of-plumb masonry and walls with rebar.

Mounting in six steps

The articulated bracket is installed with three tools: a hammer drill, spanners and a spirit level.

  1. 1

    Marking and drilling

    Mark the position on the wall and drill with the hammer drill, using the slotted holes of the fixing element.

  2. 2

    Fixing to the wall

    Fix the fixing element with the anchors and, before tightening, check with the spirit level that it is vertical.

  3. 3

    Setting the angle

    Turn the support element about the articulated joint to take up walls that protrude or recede.

  4. 4

    Locking

    Tighten the clamping element in the slot to lock the set angle permanently.

  5. 5

    Adjusting and cutting

    Adjust the lengthwise position of the support and, if necessary, cut it to size as the mounting requires.

  6. 6

    Fixing the component

    Connect the component — sill, door operator or guide rail — to the connection holes and check the final alignment with the spirit level.

Uneven walls: FAQ

How are counterweight guide rails fixed to irregular walls?

With an adjustable support (SU, SD or SC) and the SG joining bracket: the support is fixed to the wall and the joining bracket slides on it within the extension range given for each configuration, with a 35 mm overlap flap. The SD support has an offset base, for more freedom of positioning on irregular walls.

How is a wall that is not square to the landing door taken up?

With the articulated joint of the landing door brackets: the support element turns relative to the one fixed to the wall, by ± 8° on the 65 series and ± 7° on the 45 and 37 series, and a clamping element locks the angle once it is set.

Which tools are needed for mounting?

Three: a hammer drill, spanners and a spirit level.

What if the size I need is not on the price list?

It is made to order: the projection “A” of the SC 50 170 support is defined on the site’s shop drawing. Send the drawing or the measurements to info@panevascensori.it and receive a quote for the job.
