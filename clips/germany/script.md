## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Germany — four-time World Champions and a country of fairytales. Storybook castles
and soaring cathedrals, historic gates, dark forests and lively beer halls.
Efficient, beautiful and steeped in history. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you
have to see in Germany. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Neuschwanstein, Brandenburg Gate, Rhine castles, black-red-gold flags.*

### #10 — The Berlin Wall & East Side Gallery
We start where history was made. For 28 years the Berlin Wall divided the city;
today its longest surviving stretch is the East Side Gallery, painted by artists
into a 1.3-kilometre open-air mural. A powerful, moving symbol of division and
freedom.
*B-roll: painted wall murals, the famous "fraternal kiss," visitors.*

### #9 — The Black Forest
Deep, dark and full of legend. The Black Forest in the southwest is a land of
dense pine woods, cuckoo clocks, half-timbered villages and waterfalls — the
landscape that inspired the Brothers Grimm. Drive its scenic roads and taste a
slice of real Black Forest gâteau.
*B-roll: misty pine forest, half-timbered villages, cuckoo clocks.*

### #8 — Marienplatz, Munich
The heart of Bavaria. Munich's central square is dominated by the ornate New Town
Hall, whose Glockenspiel clock springs to life with dancing figures each day.
Nearby are the beer halls and, each autumn, the world-famous Oktoberfest. Bavarian
spirit at its best.
*B-roll: New Town Hall, Glockenspiel figures, beer hall.*

### #7 — The Holstentor, Lübeck
A medieval brick gateway. The twin-towered Holstentor once guarded the wealthy
trading city of Lübeck and leans slightly with age. Behind it lies a UNESCO old
town of red-brick Gothic churches and marzipan shops — the jewel of the northern
Hanseatic coast.
*B-roll: leaning brick gate, old town spires, harbour.*

### #6 — Heidelberg Castle
Romantic ruins above the river. The red-stone ruins of Heidelberg Castle sit on a
hillside overlooking the Neckar river and one of Germany's prettiest old towns.
Inside is the world's largest wine barrel. A favourite of poets and painters for
centuries.
*B-roll: hillside castle ruins, old town and river below, giant wine barrel.*

### #5 — The Reichstag, Berlin
The home of German democracy. The historic Reichstag parliament building is
crowned by a stunning modern glass dome you can climb, spiralling up for views
across Berlin while looking down into the chamber below — a symbol of openness and
unity.
*B-roll: glass dome interior spiral, city view, building façade.*

### #4 — The Romantic Rhine
A river lined with castles. The Middle Rhine Valley winds between steep vineyards
crowned by dozens of medieval castles and the legendary Lorelei rock. Cruise it by
boat and watch fortress after fortress slide past above the water — pure German
romance.
*B-roll: river cruise, hilltop castles, terraced vineyards, the Lorelei.*

### #3 — Cologne Cathedral
A Gothic giant. Cologne Cathedral took over 600 years to complete and its twin
spires soar 157 metres into the sky — once the tallest building in the world.
Survivor of wartime bombing, it dominates the city skyline right beside the Rhine.
Climb its tower if you dare.
*B-roll: twin spires, cathedral interior, view from the top.*

### #2 — The Brandenburg Gate, Berlin
The symbol of a united Germany. This neoclassical triumphal gate, crowned by its
chariot and horses, has watched over Berlin's greatest moments — from division to
the joyous reunification of the nation. Today it stands proudly at the heart of the
capital.
*B-roll: the gate by day, lit at night, crowds in the square.*

### #1 — Neuschwanstein Castle
And our number one is the fairytale castle that inspired Disney. Built by the
"mad" King Ludwig II in the 19th century, Neuschwanstein rises with white towers
and turrets against the Bavarian Alps, perfectly framed from the Marienbrücke
bridge. It looks too magical to be real. If you see one place in Germany, make it
this.
*B-roll: castle on its crag, alpine backdrop, view from the bridge, interior.*
