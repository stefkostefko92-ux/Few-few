## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Saudi Arabia — host of the 2034 World Cup and a country opening up to travellers
for the first time. Ancient carved tombs and vast deserts, dramatic cliffs,
mountain villages and futuristic cities. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to
see in Saudi Arabia. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — AlUla tombs, desert dunes, Riyadh skyline, green flags.*

### #10 — Kingdom Centre, Riyadh
We start in the capital. The Kingdom Centre tower soars over Riyadh, its distinctive
top opening into a curved "bottle" shape spanned by a Sky Bridge. Step out onto the
bridge for sweeping views over the modern, fast-growing city.
*B-roll: the tower, Sky Bridge view, city skyline at night.*

### #9 — Diriyah
The birthplace of the kingdom. On the edge of Riyadh, the mud-brick UNESCO city of
At-Turaif in Diriyah was the original Saudi capital, its restored earthen palaces
and walls glowing at sunset. Where Saudi history began.
*B-roll: mud-brick old city, palm groves, sunset glow.*

### #8 — Jeddah Corniche & Old Town
The gateway by the Red Sea. Jeddah blends a buzzing seaside Corniche — with the
world's tallest fountain — and the UNESCO old town of Al-Balad, a maze of coral-
stone houses with beautiful carved wooden balconies. Old and new side by side.
*B-roll: Corniche fountain, coral-stone houses, wooden balconies.*

### #7 — The Farasan Islands
A Red Sea secret. This coral archipelago off the southwest coast offers turquoise
waters, untouched beaches, mangroves and rich marine life — a quiet paradise for
snorkelling and birdwatching.
*B-roll: turquoise water, empty beaches, coral, birds.*

### #6 — Rijal Almaa
A village of stone towers. Tucked in the green mountains of the southwest, this
beautifully preserved heritage village is built from stone and slate in tall,
fortress-like houses, some decorated with colourful painted interiors. Unique and
atmospheric.
*B-roll: stone tower houses, mountain setting, painted rooms.*

### #5 — The Asir Mountains & Abha
A surprisingly green Arabia. Around the city of Abha, the high Asir Mountains are
cool, misty and terraced with farms — a world away from the desert, with cable cars,
flower-decked villages and dramatic cliff views.
*B-roll: misty green mountains, terraces, cable car, cliffs.*

### #4 — The Empty Quarter (Rub' al Khali)
The largest sand desert on Earth. The Rub' al Khali is an endless sea of giant
dunes rolling to the horizon — vast, silent and awe-inspiring. Camp beneath a sky
ablaze with stars in one of the world's last great wildernesses.
*B-roll: giant dunes, 4x4 on the sand, desert camp, stars.*

### #3 — The Edge of the World
A cliff at the end of the earth. Outside Riyadh, the Tuwaiq escarpment drops away in
a sheer 300-metre cliff overlooking a vast desert plain that stretches endlessly
into the haze. Standing on the edge feels like the rim of the planet.
*B-roll: sheer cliff edge, endless plain below, sunset.*

### #2 — AlUla Old Town & Elephant Rock
A magical desert oasis. Beyond Hegra, the AlUla region dazzles with its labyrinth
old town of mud-brick houses, dramatic sandstone canyons and the giant natural arch
of Elephant Rock. A landscape of pure wonder.
*B-roll: AlUla old town, Elephant Rock, sandstone canyons.*

### #1 — Hegra (Madain Salih)
And our number one is Saudi Arabia's first UNESCO World Heritage Site. Hegra was the
southern city of the Nabataeans — the same people who built Petra — and its 100-plus
monumental tombs are carved directly into towering sandstone outcrops in the desert,
incredibly preserved after 2,000 years. Majestic and serene. If you see one thing in
Saudi Arabia, make it this.
*B-roll: carved tombs in the desert, façades up close, dunes, golden light.*
