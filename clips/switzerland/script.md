## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Switzerland — a country that looks like it was designed for postcards. Snow-capped
Alps and glacier trains, turquoise lakes and storybook towns, chocolate, cheese and
fairytale castles. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Switzerland.
Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Matterhorn, alpine train, lakes, white-cross flags.*

### #10 — The Old Town of Bern
We start in the capital. Bern's medieval old town, wrapped in a bend of the river
Aare, is a UNESCO gem of six kilometres of covered arcades, painted fountains and
the famous Zytglogge clock tower. Charming, walkable and beautifully preserved.
*B-roll: arcaded streets, Zytglogge clock, river bend from above.*

### #9 — The Rhine Falls
The most powerful waterfall in Europe. Near Schaffhausen, the Rhine Falls thunder
150 metres wide over a rocky drop, with boats that take you to a rock right in the
middle of the cascade. Raw natural power in gentle Switzerland.
*B-roll: wide falls, boat to the central rock, castle viewpoint.*

### #8 — Chillon Castle, Lake Geneva
A fairytale fortress on the water. Château de Chillon sits on a rock at the edge of
Lake Geneva, framed by mountains, its towers reflected in the still water. One of
the most visited and most romantic castles in Switzerland.
*B-roll: lakeside castle, courtyards, mountain reflection.*

### #7 — The Glacier Express
The world's slowest express train — and one of its most scenic. The Glacier
Express winds for nearly eight hours between Zermatt and St Moritz, crossing 291
bridges and dramatic viaducts through the heart of the Alps, all beneath
panoramic glass roofs.
*B-roll: red train on a curved viaduct, snowy peaks through glass roof.*

### #6 — The Lavaux Vineyards
Terraced wine country by the lake. Above Lake Geneva, the Lavaux vineyards climb
the steep slopes in centuries-old stone terraces — a UNESCO landscape best enjoyed
on a sunlit walk with the lake and Alps spread out below.
*B-roll: terraced vines, lake and Alps backdrop, village paths.*

### #5 — Lake Lucerne & the Chapel Bridge
Quintessential Switzerland. The medieval Chapel Bridge — a covered wooden bridge
with a stone water tower — crosses the river in Lucerne, a beautiful town set on a
mountain-ringed lake. Take a paddle-steamer cruise for the full storybook effect.
*B-roll: Chapel Bridge, painted gables, lake steamer, Mt Pilatus behind.*

### #4 — The Lauterbrunnen Valley
A valley of 72 waterfalls. Lauterbrunnen is a deep glacial valley where sheer
cliffs drop to a green floor laced with cascades — most famously the Staubbach
Falls plunging straight off the rock. Said to have inspired Tolkien's Rivendell.
*B-roll: waterfall off a cliff, valley floor, traditional chalets.*

### #3 — Jungfraujoch, the Top of Europe
The highest railway station in Europe. A cog railway climbs through the mountain to
Jungfraujoch at 3,454 metres, where you step out onto an eternal world of snow,
ice palaces and the vast Aletsch Glacier. Unforgettable alpine grandeur.
*B-roll: cog railway, snowy plateau, ice palace, glacier view.*

### #2 — Interlaken & the Bernese Oberland
The adventure heart of the Alps. Set between two brilliant blue lakes, Interlaken
is the gateway to the giant peaks of the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau — and a hub for
paragliding, hiking and skydiving over some of the most beautiful scenery on Earth.
*B-roll: paraglider over the lakes, blue water, the three peaks.*

### #1 — The Matterhorn & Zermatt
And our number one is the most famous mountain in the world. The Matterhorn's near-
perfect pyramid towers 4,478 metres over the car-free village of Zermatt. Watch it
glow pink at sunrise, ride the Gornergrat railway for the classic view, and you'll
understand why it's the symbol of Switzerland. If you see one thing here, make it
this.
*B-roll: Matterhorn at sunrise, Zermatt village, Gornergrat railway, reflection.*

Switzerland. Which one would you visit first? Subscribe — we're
