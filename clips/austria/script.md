## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Austria — the land of imperial palaces and alpine magic. Grand Habsburg cities,
mirror-still mountain lakes, the music of Mozart and the hills of The Sound of
Music. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Austria. Let's count them
down.
*B-roll: montage — Hallstatt, Schönbrunn, alpine peaks, red-white-red flags.*

### #10 — The Grossglockner High Alpine Road
We start on one of Europe's greatest drives. This winding mountain road climbs in
dramatic hairpins past glaciers and meadows to the foot of the Grossglockner,
Austria's highest peak, with breathtaking views and marmots along the way.
*B-roll: hairpin road, glacier viewpoint, alpine meadows.*

### #9 — Innsbruck & the Nordkette
A city in the arms of the Alps. Innsbruck blends a colourful old town — crowned by
the famous Golden Roof — with a funicular and cable car that whisk you from the
centre to high alpine peaks in minutes. Mountains right on the doorstep.
*B-roll: Golden Roof, old town, cable car to snowy ridges.*

### #8 — Melk Abbey & the Wachau Valley
Baroque splendour above the Danube. The vast yellow Melk Abbey sits high over the
river, its library and golden church among the finest baroque interiors in Europe.
Below stretches the Wachau, a UNESCO valley of vineyards, apricot orchards and
river castles.
*B-roll: hilltop abbey, golden church, Danube vineyards.*

### #7 — Salzburg's Old Town
The birthplace of Mozart. Salzburg's baroque old town is a UNESCO maze of domed
churches, elegant squares and the boutique-lined Getreidegasse, all set against
green hills. The backdrop to both Mozart and The Sound of Music.
*B-roll: old town domes, Getreidegasse, Mirabell gardens.*

### #6 — Hohensalzburg Fortress
One of the largest medieval castles in Europe. Perched on a hill above Salzburg,
this 900-year-old fortress is beautifully preserved and offers commanding views
over the city's spires and the mountains beyond. Reach it by a charming
funicular.
*B-roll: hilltop fortress, ramparts, panorama over Salzburg.*

### #5 — The Eisriesenwelt Ice Caves
The largest ice cave system in the world. Inside a mountain near Werfen lies a
frozen wonderland of glittering ice formations, sculpted halls and frozen
waterfalls, explored by lamplight. Otherworldly and dramatic.
*B-roll: cliffside cave entrance, ice formations by lamplight.*

### #4 — St. Stephen's Cathedral, Vienna
The soul of the capital. "Steffl" rises in the heart of Vienna with its soaring
Gothic spire and dazzling patterned tiled roof. Climb the tower or descend into the
catacombs of this 700-year-old cathedral at the very centre of the city.
*B-roll: tiled roof, Gothic spire, cathedral interior.*

### #3 — Hallstatt
The most photographed village in Austria. Hallstatt clings to a narrow shore
between a mountain and a mirror-still lake, its pastel houses and slender church
spire reflected in the water. So picture-perfect it's been copied wholesale in
China. Pure fairytale.
*B-roll: lakeside village reflection, narrow lanes, salt-mine viewpoint.*

### #2 — Vienna's Ringstrasse & the Hofburg
Imperial grandeur. The grand Ringstrasse boulevard loops the old city past the
State Opera, grand museums and parliament, leading to the Hofburg — the sprawling
former palace of the Habsburg emperors. Add coffee houses and waltzes and you have
the elegant capital of an empire.
*B-roll: Ringstrasse, opera house, Hofburg courtyards, horse carriages.*

### #1 — Schönbrunn Palace, Vienna
And our number one is Austria's answer to Versailles. The 1,400-room Schönbrunn
was the summer palace of the Habsburgs, with sumptuous imperial rooms, vast formal
gardens climbing to the Gloriette arch, and the world's oldest zoo. Grand,
golden and unforgettable. If you see one place in Austria, make it this.
*B-roll: yellow palace façade, gardens, the Gloriette, Great Gallery interior.*
