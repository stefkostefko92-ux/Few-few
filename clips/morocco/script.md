## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Morocco — semi-finalists in 2022, a 2030 World Cup co-host, and one of the most
spellbinding countries on Earth. Blue-painted mountain towns and golden Sahara
dunes, labyrinth medinas, grand mosques and snow-capped Atlas peaks. Here are the
Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Morocco. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Marrakech medina, blue Chefchaouen, Sahara camels, red flags.*

### #10 — Volubilis
We start with ancient Rome in Africa. Volubilis is a remarkably preserved Roman
city near Meknes, with standing columns, triumphal arches and stunning floor
mosaics still in place after nearly 2,000 years. A UNESCO window into the empire's
southern frontier.
*B-roll: Roman columns, mosaics, triumphal arch, rolling fields.*

### #9 — Aït Benhaddou
A fortified city of earth. This UNESCO ksar is a cluster of reddish mud-brick
kasbahs rising from the desert along an old caravan route. Its dramatic look has
made it a backdrop for countless films — from Gladiator to Game of Thrones.
*B-roll: mud-brick fortress, river crossing, towers at sunset.*

### #8 — The Hassan II Mosque, Casablanca
One of the largest mosques in the world. Rising right on the Atlantic shore, the
Hassan II Mosque has a minaret soaring 210 metres and can hold over 100,000
worshippers, with parts of its floor built out over the sea. A masterpiece of
Moroccan craftsmanship.
*B-roll: minaret over the ocean, vast courtyard, intricate tilework.*

### #7 — The Atlas Mountains & Todra Gorge
Morocco's rugged spine. The High Atlas rises with snow-capped peaks, Berber
villages and terraced valleys. Among its wonders is the Todra Gorge, where sheer
rock walls tower 300 metres over a narrow river canyon. Spectacular hiking country.
*B-roll: snowy peaks, Berber village, sheer gorge walls.*

### #6 — Bahia Palace & the Gardens of Marrakech
A vision of Moroccan elegance. The 19th-century Bahia Palace dazzles with carved
cedar ceilings, zellige tilework and tranquil courtyards. Nearby, the cobalt-blue
Jardin Majorelle offers a cool, exotic escape from the city heat.
*B-roll: carved ceilings, tiled courtyards, blue Majorelle garden.*

### #5 — Fes el-Bali
The world's largest car-free urban area. The ancient medina of Fes is a
bewildering maze of 9,000 alleys, home to the world's oldest university and the
famous Chouara tanneries, where leather is still dyed in stone vats exactly as it
was centuries ago. A living medieval city.
*B-roll: crowded alleys, colourful tannery pits, artisans at work.*

### #4 — Chefchaouen
The famous blue city. Tucked in the Rif Mountains, Chefchaouen's old town is
painted in every shade of blue — walls, steps and doorways — making it one of the
most photogenic places on the planet. Wonderfully relaxed and utterly unique.
*B-roll: blue-washed lanes, flower pots, hillside view of the town.*

### #3 — The Sahara at Erg Chebbi
The desert of dreams. Near Merzouga, the towering golden dunes of Erg Chebbi rise
up to 150 metres. Ride a camel over the sand, sleep in a desert camp, and watch the
sun set and the stars blaze over the endless Sahara. Unforgettable.
*B-roll: camel caravan over dunes, desert camp, starry sky, sunrise.*

### #2 — Koutoubia Mosque & Jemaa el-Fnaa
The beating heart of Marrakech. The Koutoubia's 77-metre minaret towers over the
city, beside the legendary Jemaa el-Fnaa square — which by night transforms into a
swirl of food stalls, musicians, storytellers and lantern light. Pure sensory
overload.
*B-roll: Koutoubia minaret, square at dusk, food stalls, performers.*

### #1 — The Medina of Marrakech
And our number one is Marrakech itself. The "Red City's" UNESCO medina is a
dazzling labyrinth of souks piled with spices, lanterns, carpets and leather, dotted
with hidden riads, palaces and gardens behind earthen walls. Chaotic, colourful and
intoxicating. If you see one place in Morocco, make it this.
*B-roll: souk colours, spice pyramids, riad courtyard, ramparts at sunset.*
