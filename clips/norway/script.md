## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Norway — a land carved by ice and sea. Towering fjords and plunging waterfalls,
dramatic cliffs, the northern lights in winter and the midnight sun in summer.
Wild, pure and unforgettable. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in
Norway. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — fjords, Trolltunga, northern lights, red-white-blue flags.*

### #10 — The Oslo Opera House
We start in the capital with a building you can climb. Oslo's striking white Opera
House rises out of the fjord like a glacier, with a sloping marble roof that lets
you walk right up to the top for views over the city and water. Bold, modern
Norway.
*B-roll: sloping roof, people walking up, fjord and skyline.*

### #9 — Bryggen, Bergen
A colourful Hanseatic harbour. Bergen's Bryggen wharf is a UNESCO row of crooked,
brightly painted wooden trading houses dating back centuries — the photogenic heart
of Norway's beautiful "gateway to the fjords."
*B-roll: colourful wooden wharf, harbour, fish market, funicular view.*

### #8 — The Flåm Railway
One of the world's most scenic train rides. The Flåm Railway descends a steep
mountain valley in a series of spirals, past thundering waterfalls and sheer cliffs
down to the edge of a fjord. An engineering marvel through breathtaking scenery.
*B-roll: train on the mountainside, Kjosfossen waterfall stop, fjord below.*

### #7 — The Atlantic Ocean Road
A road that dances with the sea. This famous stretch hops across small islands and
skerries on a series of dramatic bridges — including the gravity-defying
Storseisundet — right at the edge of the wild Atlantic. Spectacular in any weather.
*B-roll: curving bridge over the sea, waves, aerial of the road.*

### #6 — The Lofoten Islands
Jagged peaks rising straight from the sea. The Lofoten archipelago, above the
Arctic Circle, is a string of dramatic mountains, red fishing huts and white sand
beaches with turquoise water. Stunning under the midnight sun or the northern
lights.
*B-roll: red rorbu huts, peaks over the sea, white beach, aurora.*

### #5 — The North Cape & the Midnight Sun
The edge of Europe. Nordkapp is a sheer cliff at the very top of the continent,
where in summer the sun never sets and you can stand beneath the globe monument
gazing out over the Arctic Ocean. The end of the road, in the best way.
*B-roll: cliff and globe monument, midnight sun over the ocean.*

### #4 — Pulpit Rock (Preikestolen)
One of the world's most famous viewpoints. Preikestolen is a flat cliff that juts
out 600 metres straight above the Lysefjord. The hike rewards you with a
heart-stopping view from the edge of this natural platform. Pure Norwegian drama.
*B-roll: the flat cliff over the fjord, hikers on the edge, fjord far below.*

### #3 — Trolltunga
The "Troll's Tongue." Trolltunga is a thin sliver of rock jutting horizontally out
of a mountainside, suspended 700 metres above a glacial lake. The long hike to
reach it is tough — but standing on that ledge is one of the most jaw-dropping
photos in the world.
*B-roll: the rock ledge over the lake, hiker at the tip, mountain panorama.*

### #2 — Geirangerfjord
The most beautiful fjord of all. A UNESCO World Heritage Site, Geirangerfjord winds
between sheer green walls laced with waterfalls like the Seven Sisters, with farms
clinging impossibly to the cliffs. Cruise it by boat or admire it from the
dizzying Dalsnibba viewpoint.
*B-roll: fjord cruise, Seven Sisters falls, Dalsnibba viewpoint.*

### #1 — The Norwegian Fjords & Northern Lights
And our number one isn't a single spot — it's Norway itself. The fjords are among
the natural wonders of the world: deep blue arms of the sea reaching far inland
between towering cliffs. And in the dark Arctic winter, the northern lights dance
green and purple across the sky above them. There is nothing else like it on
Earth.
*B-roll: epic fjord aerials, aurora over a fjord, reflections, snow.*
