## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Colombia — a country bursting with colour, rhythm and warmth. From Caribbean
beaches to snow-capped Andes, colonial walled cities to a river that runs in five
colours, it's one of South America's most surprising destinations. Here are the
Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Colombia. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Cartagena streets, green valleys, colourful river, yellow flags.*

### #10 — Comuna 13 (Medellín)
We start with a story of transformation. Once one of the most dangerous
neighbourhoods in the world, Medellín's Comuna 13 is now a vibrant open-air
gallery of street art, music and outdoor escalators that climb the hillside.
Local guides tell its remarkable story of renewal. It's the new face of Colombia.
*B-roll: hillside murals, outdoor escalators, street performers.*

### #9 — Las Lajas Sanctuary (Nariño)
A church built inside a canyon. Las Lajas is a stunning neo-Gothic basilica that
rises on a bridge spanning a deep river gorge near the Ecuadorian border. With its
spires emerging from the cliffs above the rushing water, it's one of the most
spectacularly located churches in the world.
*B-roll: basilica spanning the gorge, river below, bridge approach.*

### #8 — Tayrona National Park
Where the jungle meets the Caribbean. Tayrona's coastline is a string of golden
coves backed by dense rainforest and giant boulders, with the Sierra Nevada
mountains rising behind. Hike through the jungle to beaches like Cabo San Juan —
postcard-perfect and gloriously wild.
*B-roll: jungle-backed beach, boulders, hikers on the trail.*

### #7 — Guatapé & El Peñón
The most colourful town in Colombia, beside one of its most famous rocks. Guatapé
is lined with houses decorated in bright painted reliefs called zócalos. Next to
it rises El Peñón de Guatapé, a 200-metre monolith — climb its 700 steps for a
breathtaking view over a maze of blue lakes and islands.
*B-roll: colourful zócalos, the rock with its zig-zag stairs, lake view from top.*

### #6 — The Coffee Cultural Landscape
The heart of Colombian coffee. This UNESCO-listed region of rolling green hills is
covered in coffee plantations dotted with palm trees and colourful farmhouses.
Tour a finca, learn how the world's favourite beans are grown and picked, and
soak up the laid-back rhythm of the Coffee Triangle.
*B-roll: rolling coffee hills, beans being picked, colonial fincas.*

### #5 — Cocora Valley
Home to the tallest palm trees on Earth. In the Cocora Valley, the wax palms —
Colombia's national tree — soar up to 60 metres into the misty green hills. Hiking
or horseback riding among these impossibly tall palms feels like stepping into a
fairytale.
*B-roll: towering wax palms in mist, hikers, green valley.*

### #4 — San Agustín
Colombia's great archaeological mystery. Scattered across green hills are hundreds
of mysterious stone statues carved by a civilisation that vanished long ago. This
UNESCO site holds the largest group of religious monuments and megalithic
sculptures in South America — haunting and little-visited.
*B-roll: carved stone figures, burial mounds, green hills.*

### #3 — Ciudad Perdida (the Lost City)
Older than Machu Picchu and hidden deep in the jungle. Ciudad Perdida is an
ancient terraced city of the Tayrona people, reachable only by a multi-day trek
through the Sierra Nevada. Climbing its 1,200 mossy stone steps to the summit
terraces is one of the great adventures in South America.
*B-roll: jungle trek, stone terraces, river crossings.*

### #2 — Caño Cristales
Often called the most beautiful river in the world. For a few months each year,
an aquatic plant turns the "River of Five Colours" into flowing ribbons of vivid
red, alongside yellows, greens and blues over the riverbed. It's a natural
phenomenon found almost nowhere else on Earth.
*B-roll: red river over rock, waterfalls, natural pools.*

### #1 — The Walled City of Cartagena
And our number one is Colombia's crown jewel. Cartagena's old town is a UNESCO
World Heritage Site wrapped in centuries-old stone walls, with balconies dripping
in bougainvillea, golden-lit plazas and the mighty San Felipe fortress standing
guard. By day it glows; by night it dances. If you see one place in Colombia, make
it this.
*B-roll: walled streets, bougainvillea balconies, San Felipe fortress, sunset.*
