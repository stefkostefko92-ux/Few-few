## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Scotland — wild, misty and steeped in legend. Brooding castles and dramatic
highlands, deep dark lochs, ancient stone and a monster that may or may not exist.
Romantic and unforgettable. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in
Scotland. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — highland glens, castles, lochs, Saltire flags.*

### #10 — Stirling Castle
We start at one of Scotland's grandest fortresses. Perched on a volcanic crag,
Stirling Castle guarded the gateway to the Highlands and was the childhood home of
Mary, Queen of Scots. Below it lies the battlefield where Scotland won its
independence at Bannockburn.
*B-roll: hilltop castle, Great Hall, view over the plains.*

### #9 — Loch Ness & Urquhart Castle
Home of a legend. Loch Ness is a vast, deep, dark lake said to hide the
mysterious monster "Nessie." On its shore stand the romantic ruins of Urquhart
Castle, gazing out over the water. Cruise the loch and keep your camera ready —
just in case.
*B-roll: dark loch, ruined castle on the shore, boat cruise.*

### #8 — The Kelpies
Giant horses of steel. Near Falkirk, the Kelpies are two 30-metre-high horse-head
sculptures — the largest equine artworks in the world — gleaming beside a canal.
Inspired by the mythical water-horses of Scottish legend, they're stunning lit up
at night.
*B-roll: the two steel horse heads, reflection, night lighting.*

### #7 — Edinburgh's Old Town & the Royal Mile
A medieval maze. The Royal Mile runs through the heart of Edinburgh's UNESCO Old
Town, a steep ridge of cobbled streets, hidden closes and towering tenements
linking the castle to the royal Palace of Holyroodhouse. Atmospheric and full of
history.
*B-roll: Royal Mile, narrow closes, tenements, street performers.*

### #6 — Ben Nevis
The roof of Britain. Ben Nevis, near Fort William, is the highest mountain in the
British Isles at 1,345 metres. Climbers tackle its slopes for sweeping highland
views, while its sheer north face is a legendary challenge for serious
mountaineers.
*B-roll: the mountain, hikers on the path, summit views.*

### #5 — The Isle of Skye
The most magical of the Scottish islands. Skye is a land of jagged peaks, fairy
pools and surreal rock formations like the Old Man of Storr and the Quiraing.
Mist drifts over dramatic coastlines and lochs. Pure Highland enchantment.
*B-roll: Old Man of Storr, fairy pools, Quiraing ridge, coastline.*

### #4 — The Glenfinnan Viaduct
The famous railway arc. This curving 21-arch viaduct sweeps across a Highland
glen — and became world-famous as the bridge the Hogwarts Express crosses in the
Harry Potter films. Time your visit for the steam train and it's pure movie magic.
*B-roll: steam train crossing the curved viaduct, glen and loch below.*

### #3 — Glencoe
The most dramatic glen in Scotland. Glencoe is a steep-sided valley of brooding
peaks, waterfalls and big skies, soaked in both beauty and a dark history. A
favourite of hikers, photographers and filmmakers alike — raw Highland grandeur.
*B-roll: the valley peaks, waterfalls, moody clouds, lone road.*

### #2 — Eilean Donan Castle
Perhaps the most photographed castle in Scotland. Eilean Donan sits on its own
small island where three lochs meet, connected to the shore by an arched stone
bridge and framed by mountains. Romantic, ancient and impossibly picturesque.
*B-roll: castle on the islet, stone bridge, mountain reflection at dusk.*

### #1 — Edinburgh Castle
And our number one towers over the capital. Edinburgh Castle sits on an ancient
volcanic rock at the top of the Royal Mile, guarding the city for nearly a
thousand years. Home to the Scottish Crown Jewels and the famous One O'Clock Gun,
with sweeping views over Edinburgh. If you see one place in Scotland, make it this.
*B-roll: castle on its rock, esplanade, crown jewels, city panorama.*
