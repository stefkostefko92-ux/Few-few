## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
In 2026, Canada co-hosts the World Cup with matches in Toronto and Vancouver. But
this is the second-largest country on the planet — a land of turquoise glacial
lakes, thundering falls, polished skylines and mountains that stop you in your
tracks. Before the whistle blows, here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see
in Canada. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — turquoise lake, CN Tower, falls, fans with Canadian flags.*

### #10 — Parliament Hill (Ottawa)
We start in the capital. Parliament Hill overlooks the Ottawa River with its
dramatic Gothic Revival buildings and the iconic Peace Tower rising above them.
In summer, the changing of the guard fills the lawns; at night, light shows play
across the stone. It's the political and architectural heart of Canada.
*B-roll: Peace Tower, changing of the guard, river view.*

### #9 — Old Québec City
Step into Europe without leaving North America. Old Québec is the only walled
city north of Mexico, a UNESCO gem of cobblestone streets, French cafés and the
fairytale Château Frontenac — said to be the most photographed hotel in the
world. Cosy in winter snow, lively in summer, it's pure old-world charm.
*B-roll: Château Frontenac, cobblestone lanes, city walls.*

### #8 — Stanley Park & Vancouver
On the Pacific coast, World Cup host city Vancouver wraps a glass skyline around
the sea and the mountains. At its edge lies Stanley Park — a thousand acres of
rainforest, totem poles and a seawall path with views of the bay. Few cities mix
nature and skyline this beautifully.
*B-roll: seawall, totem poles, skyline with mountains behind.*

### #7 — The Icefields Parkway (Alberta)
One of the most scenic drives on Earth. The Icefields Parkway threads between
Banff and Jasper past glaciers, waterfalls and a string of impossibly blue lakes.
The highlight is the Columbia Icefield, where you can walk on a glacier thousands
of years old. Every bend reveals another postcard.
*B-roll: winding mountain road, glacier walk, roadside lakes.*

### #6 — Niagara Falls (Ontario)
On the Canadian side, Horseshoe Falls is the largest and most spectacular of the
Niagara Falls — a curtain of water more than 50 metres high and 600 metres wide.
Ride a boat into the mist or watch it lit up in colour at night. It's pure,
thundering natural power, an easy trip from Toronto.
*B-roll: Horseshoe Falls aerial, boat in the spray, night illumination.*

### #5 — Lake Louise (Alberta)
A lake so perfectly turquoise it looks painted. Fed by glaciers, Lake Louise sits
beneath snow-capped peaks and the Victoria Glacier, mirrored on its still
surface. Paddle a canoe across it in summer or skate on it in winter — either way
it's one of the most beautiful lakes in the world.
*B-roll: canoe on turquoise water, glacier backdrop, reflections.*

### #4 — CN Tower (Toronto)
The icon of Canada's biggest city and a World Cup host. For decades the CN Tower
was the tallest free-standing structure on Earth, soaring 553 metres over Toronto.
Stand on its glass floor, dine in the revolving restaurant, or — if you dare —
walk hands-free around the edge on the EdgeWalk. The skyline views are unmatched.
*B-roll: tower against skyline, glass floor look-down, EdgeWalk.*

### #3 — Moraine Lake (Alberta)
Perhaps the most photographed view in all of Canada. Moraine Lake glows an
electric blue beneath the jagged Valley of the Ten Peaks, the colour coming from
glacial rock flour in the water. The view from the Rockpile is so iconic it once
featured on the Canadian twenty-dollar bill. Simply breathtaking.
*B-roll: Rockpile viewpoint, ten peaks reflection, canoe on the lake.*

### #2 — Whistler (British Columbia)
North of Vancouver lies one of the world's great mountain resorts. Whistler
hosted the 2010 Winter Olympics and draws skiers and snowboarders from around the
globe in winter, then mountain bikers and hikers in summer. Ride the Peak 2 Peak
gondola between two mountaintops for views that go on forever.
*B-roll: gondola over the valley, ski runs, alpine village.*

### #1 — Banff National Park & the Canadian Rockies
And our number one is Canada's crown jewel. Banff was the country's first national
park, a vast wilderness of jagged peaks, turquoise rivers and emerald lakes in the
heart of the Rockies. Soak in hot springs with a mountain view, ride the gondola
up Sulphur Mountain, and spot elk, bears and bighorn sheep. If you see one place
in Canada, make it the Rockies.
*B-roll: Banff townsite, Sulphur Mountain gondola, wildlife, mountain panorama.*

of Churchill, Manitoba; and the dramatic coastline of Cape Breton's Cabot Trail.
