## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Belgium — small, but bursting with charm. Medieval guildhalls and fairytale
canals, world-famous chocolate and beer, and a sense of humour all its own. Here
are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Belgium. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Grand-Place, Bruges canals, chocolate shops, black-yellow-red flags.*

### #10 — The Atomium, Brussels
We start with Belgium's space-age icon. Built for the 1958 World's Fair, the
Atomium is a giant model of an iron crystal — nine shining spheres connected by
tubes, magnified 165 billion times. Ride up inside for panoramic views of the
capital.
*B-roll: shining spheres, escalators inside the tubes, city view.*

### #9 — Dinant
A town squeezed dramatically between cliff and river. Dinant sits beneath a
clifftop citadel, its bulb-domed church right on the Meuse. It's also the
birthplace of Adolphe Sax, inventor of the saxophone — celebrated with colourful
sax sculptures all over town.
*B-roll: cliffside citadel, riverside church, saxophone art.*

### #8 — Antwerp Central Station
One of the most beautiful train stations in the world. Antwerp's "railway
cathedral" dazzles with a soaring stone façade, a vast domed hall and gilded
detailing. The city beyond is the global capital of diamonds and home to the
painter Rubens.
*B-roll: grand station hall, the dome, platforms below.*

### #7 — The Cinquantenaire & Royal Quarter, Brussels
Grand and ceremonial Brussels. The Cinquantenaire park is crowned by a monumental
triumphal arch, surrounded by museums, near the elegant Royal Palace. It's the
stately, green heart of the capital.
*B-roll: triumphal arch, park, Royal Palace façade.*

### #6 — The Battlefields of Flanders
A place of memory. Around Ypres, the fields of Flanders saw some of the most
devastating battles of the First World War. Today the rebuilt Cloth Hall, the
Menin Gate and rows of war cemeteries with their poppies form a deeply moving
landmark of remembrance.
*B-roll: Menin Gate, poppies, Cloth Hall, cemeteries (respectful tone).*

### #5 — Ghent
A medieval powerhouse, often less crowded than its famous neighbour. Ghent lines
its rivers with grand guildhalls and is watched over by St Bavo's Cathedral — home
to the celebrated "Ghent Altarpiece" — and the brooding Gravensteen castle. Lively
and beautiful by day and night.
*B-roll: waterfront guildhalls, Gravensteen castle, lit quays at night.*

### #4 — Manneken Pis, Brussels
The cheeky little symbol of Brussels. This tiny bronze statue of a boy famously
relieving himself has become a beloved national mascot — and is dressed up in
hundreds of different costumes throughout the year. Small, silly and quintessentially
Belgian.
*B-roll: the statue, costumed versions, crowds and chocolate shops nearby.*

### #3 — The Belfry of Bruges
Climb into the Middle Ages. The 83-metre Belfry has towered over Bruges' market
square for 700 years. Conquer its 366 winding steps for a breathtaking view over
the rooftops and canals of one of Europe's best-preserved medieval cities.
*B-roll: the leaning belfry, climb up the stairs, rooftop panorama.*

### #2 — Historic Bruges
A real-life fairytale. Bruges is a maze of cobblestone streets, dreamy canals,
stone bridges and stepped-gable houses, all wonderfully preserved from its golden
age as a trading city. Glide along the canals by boat and it feels frozen in time.
The "Venice of the North."
*B-roll: canal cruise, Rozenhoedkaai view, swans, market square.*

### #1 — The Grand-Place, Brussels
And our number one is one of the most beautiful squares in the world. The
Grand-Place is ringed by ornate guildhalls dripping in gold leaf, the towering
Gothic Town Hall and the grand King's House. Every two years it's carpeted with a
million flowers. Lit up at night, it's simply stunning. If you see one place in
Belgium, make it this.
*B-roll: golden guildhalls, Town Hall spire, flower carpet, night lighting.*
