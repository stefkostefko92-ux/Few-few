## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
The Netherlands — a small country with enormous charm. Windmills and tulip fields,
canals and bicycles, masterpiece-filled museums and clever cities built below sea
level. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in the Netherlands. Let's
count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Amsterdam canals, windmills, tulip fields, orange flags.*

### #10 — The Cube Houses, Rotterdam
We start with bold modern design. Rotterdam was rebuilt after the war into a city
of daring architecture, and its tilted yellow Cube Houses — each turned 45 degrees
on its point — are the most playful example. A symbol of the city's forward-looking
spirit.
*B-roll: tilted cube houses, modern skyline, Erasmus Bridge.*

### #9 — Scheveningen & the Coast
The Dutch seaside. Just outside The Hague, Scheveningen is the country's most
famous beach resort, with a long sandy shore, a grand pier and a buzzing
promenade. Proof there's more to Holland than canals.
*B-roll: pier, wide sandy beach, promenade.*

### #8 — Delft
Blue-and-white perfection. The pretty canal town of Delft gave the world its
famous blue pottery, Delftware, and was home to the painter Vermeer. Its market
square, old church and gabled houses make it one of the most charming small towns
in the country.
*B-roll: market square, Delft pottery, canal houses.*

### #7 — Zaanse Schans
A living open-air museum of windmills. Just north of Amsterdam, Zaanse Schans
preserves working historic windmills, wooden houses and craft workshops along a
river — where you can watch sawmills and oil mills turning just as they did
centuries ago.
*B-roll: row of working windmills, wooden green houses, clog workshop.*

### #6 — Giethoorn
The "Venice of the Netherlands." Giethoorn is a fairytale village with no roads in
its old centre — just canals, thatched cottages and arched wooden bridges. Glide
through it by whisper-quiet electric boat for pure tranquillity.
*B-roll: boat on a canal, thatched cottages, wooden bridges.*

### #5 — The Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam
A treasure house of Dutch art. The grand Rijksmuseum holds the masterpieces of the
Dutch Golden Age, crowned by Rembrandt's enormous "Night Watch." The building
itself, with its great galleries and gardens, is a landmark in its own right.
*B-roll: museum façade, the Night Watch, grand galleries.*

### #4 — The Anne Frank House, Amsterdam
A deeply moving piece of history. In this canal-side house, the young Anne Frank
and her family hid from the Nazis, and where she wrote her world-famous diary.
Walking through the secret annexe behind the bookcase is a powerful, unforgettable
experience.
*B-roll: canal house exterior, the bookcase, the annexe (respectful, no interior re-creation needed).*

### #3 — Keukenhof Tulip Gardens
A springtime explosion of colour. For a few weeks each spring, Keukenhof bursts
into bloom with seven million tulips, daffodils and hyacinths in dazzling
patterns. Combined with the surrounding striped tulip fields, it's the most
colourful sight in the country.
*B-roll: tulip beds in waves of colour, flower fields from above.*

### #2 — The Windmills of Kinderdijk
The classic Dutch postcard. At Kinderdijk, 19 historic windmills stand in a row
along the canals — built centuries ago to pump water and keep the low-lying land
dry. A UNESCO site and the perfect symbol of how the Dutch tamed the water.
*B-roll: row of windmills reflected in the canal, sails turning, cyclists.*

### #1 — The Canals of Amsterdam
And our number one is the capital's glorious canal ring. Amsterdam's UNESCO-listed
canals are lined with leaning gabled merchant houses, crossed by hundreds of
bridges and best seen from the water or a bicycle. Add the bustling squares,
houseboats and golden evening light, and it's pure magic. If you see one place in
the Netherlands, make it this.
*B-roll: canal cruise, gabled houses, bridges lit at night, bicycles.*
