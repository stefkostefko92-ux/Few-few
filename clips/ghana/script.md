## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Ghana — the Black Stars, one of Africa's proudest footballing nations, and a
country of warm welcomes. Historic coastal forts, lush rainforests, golden beaches
and the living traditions of ancient kingdoms. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you
have to see in Ghana. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Accra coast, Cape Coast Castle, canopy walk, red-gold-green flags.*

### #10 — Independence Square, Accra
We start in the capital. Independence Square is a vast ceremonial plaza by the sea,
framed by the Independence Arch and the Black Star Gate — symbols of Ghana's place
as the first sub-Saharan nation to win independence. The proud heart of Accra.
*B-roll: Black Star Gate, Independence Arch, ocean backdrop.*

### #9 — Larabanga Mosque
The "Mecca of West Africa." This striking little mosque in the north is built in the
distinctive Sudanese mud-and-stick style, its white walls studded with wooden beams
and topped by pyramidal towers. One of the oldest mosques in the region.
*B-roll: white mud mosque, wooden beams, village setting.*

### #8 — Mole National Park
Ghana's premier safari. At Mole, you can walk or drive among elephants, antelope,
warthogs and baboons — sometimes watching elephants bathe from the safety of a
hilltop lodge. Big African wildlife in the savannah of the north.
*B-roll: elephants at a waterhole, antelope, savannah, lodge view.*

### #7 — Lake Volta
One of the largest man-made lakes on Earth. Created by the Akosombo Dam, Lake Volta
stretches across a huge swathe of Ghana, dotted with islands and fishing
communities. Cruise its waters or relax at lakeside retreats.
*B-roll: vast lake, fishing boats, the dam, islands.*

### #6 — Wli Waterfalls
The tallest waterfall in West Africa. Tucked near the Togo border, the Wli falls
plunge through forested cliffs alive with thousands of fruit bats. A rewarding
jungle hike leads to a refreshing swim at the base.
*B-roll: tall waterfall, forest trail, clouds of bats.*

### #5 — Kumasi & the Ashanti Kingdom
The cultural heart of Ghana. Kumasi is the seat of the powerful Ashanti kingdom,
home to the Manhyia Palace, the colourful Kejetia — one of the largest markets in
West Africa — and the craft villages where kente cloth is woven. Rich, living
heritage.
*B-roll: Manhyia Palace, busy market, kente weaving.*

### #4 — Labadi Beach & Accra
Where the city hits the sea. Labadi is Accra's most lively beach, buzzing at
weekends with music, drumming, horse rides and street food. A vibrant slice of
modern Ghanaian life along the Atlantic.
*B-roll: lively beach, drumming, horses on the sand, food stalls.*

### #3 — Nzulezo Stilt Village
A village on the water. Nzulezo is built entirely on stilts above a tranquil lake,
reached by a long canoe journey through reedy waterways. Its people have lived on
the water for generations — a unique and peaceful way of life.
*B-roll: canoe through reeds, stilt houses, lake village life.*

### #2 — Kakum National Park
Walk among the treetops. Kakum protects dense tropical rainforest, and its famous
canopy walkway — a series of rope bridges strung 40 metres above the forest floor —
lets you stroll through the treetops with the jungle spread out below. Thrilling
and beautiful.
*B-roll: canopy rope bridges, rainforest from above, visitors crossing.*

### #1 — Cape Coast & Elmina Castles
And our number one is profoundly moving. These whitewashed coastal forts are UNESCO
World Heritage Sites — and former holding points of the transatlantic slave trade,
each with its haunting "Door of No Return." Beautiful yet sombre, they are among the
most important historical sites in Africa. Ghana's most significant landmark.
*B-roll: white fort over the sea, courtyards, the Door of No Return (respectful).*

of Ghana. Which one would you visit first? Subscribe — we're counting down every
