## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Bosnia and Herzegovina — through the play-offs and into the World Cup. This small
Balkan country is one of Europe's best-kept secrets: graceful Ottoman bridges,
emerald-green rivers, wild mountains and a capital where East meets West. Here are
the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Bosnia and Herzegovina. Let's count them
down.
*B-roll: montage — Mostar bridge, Sarajevo old town, waterfalls, blue-yellow flags.*

### #10 — The Latin Bridge, Sarajevo
We start where history turned. This elegant Ottoman stone bridge over the Miljacka
river in Sarajevo is the spot where Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated in
1914 — the spark that ignited the First World War. Quiet now, but world-changing.
*B-roll: stone bridge, river, old Sarajevo facades.*

### #9 — Travnik
A town of viziers and colour. Travnik, once the seat of Ottoman governors, sits
beneath a hilltop fortress, famous for its colourful "Many-Coloured Mosque" and the
crystal-clear Plava Voda springs. The birthplace of Nobel writer Ivo Andrić.
*B-roll: hilltop fortress, decorated mosque, spring waters.*

### #8 — The Mehmed Paša Sokolović Bridge, Višegrad
A UNESCO masterpiece. This graceful 16th-century Ottoman bridge of eleven stone
arches spans the green Drina river and was immortalised in Andrić's novel "The
Bridge on the Drina." A monument to a golden age of engineering.
*B-roll: multi-arch stone bridge, green river, town behind.*

### #7 — The Pliva Waterfall, Jajce
A waterfall in the heart of town. Uniquely, Jajce has a powerful 20-metre waterfall
right where two rivers meet in the centre of the city, beneath a medieval fortress.
A rare and beautiful sight found nowhere else.
*B-roll: waterfall below the old town, fortress, mills.*

### #6 — Vrelo Bosne
The source of the Bosna river. On the edge of Sarajevo, Vrelo Bosne is a tranquil
park where springs bubble up at the foot of Mount Igman into a maze of streams,
islets and little bridges. A favourite local escape, reached by a horse-drawn
carriage along an avenue of trees.
*B-roll: clear springs, islets and bridges, horse carriage.*

### #5 — Blagaj Tekke
A monastery against a cliff. The Blagaj Tekke is a serene 16th-century dervish house
built right at the foot of a towering cliff, beside the powerful spring where the
Buna river gushes out of a cave. One of the most peaceful and photogenic spots in
the country.
*B-roll: white tekke under the cliff, emerald river from the cave.*

### #4 — Počitelj
A medieval town frozen in time. Tumbling up a hillside above the Neretva river,
Počitelj is a beautifully preserved Ottoman-era village of stone houses, a mosque
and a fortress tower, all framed by pomegranate trees. Stepping back centuries.
*B-roll: stone hillside town, fortress tower, river below.*

### #3 — Kravica Waterfall
Bosnia's natural wonder. Kravica is a wide, horseshoe-shaped curtain of waterfalls
plunging 25 metres into an emerald pool surrounded by greenery — perfect for a
summer swim. Often called a mini-Niagara, it's stunning.
*B-roll: wide curtain of falls, emerald pool, swimmers.*

### #2 — Baščaršija, Sarajevo
The soul of the capital. Baščaršija is Sarajevo's old bazaar — a warren of
cobblestone lanes, copper workshops, cafés and mosques centred on the wooden
Sebilj fountain. Here a single street shifts from Ottoman East to Austro-Hungarian
West, earning Sarajevo its name as the "Jerusalem of Europe."
*B-roll: Sebilj fountain, copper alley, mosque, café with Bosnian coffee.*

### #1 — Stari Most, Mostar
And our number one is the symbol of the whole country. The Stari Most — the "Old
Bridge" — is a breathtaking single-arch Ottoman stone bridge soaring over the
turquoise Neretva river in Mostar. Destroyed in the 1990s war and lovingly rebuilt,
it's now a UNESCO World Heritage Site where daredevils leap from its 24-metre peak
into the river below. If you see one thing in Bosnia, make it this.
*B-roll: the arched bridge over turquoise water, a diver leaping, old town, sunset.*
