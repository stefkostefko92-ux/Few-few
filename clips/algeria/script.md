## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Algeria — the largest country in Africa, and one of its least explored. Roman ruins
in remarkable condition, a vast and beautiful Sahara, dramatic canyon cities and a
sweeping Mediterranean coast. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in
Algeria. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Algiers white city, Roman ruins, Sahara, green-white-red flags.*

### #10 — Notre-Dame d'Afrique (Algiers)
We start above the capital. This striking 19th-century basilica sits on a cliff
overlooking the Bay of Algiers, blending Byzantine and Moorish styles. Its setting
and sweeping sea views make it one of the city's most beloved landmarks.
*B-roll: hilltop basilica, dome, view over the bay.*

### #9 — Constantine, the City of Bridges
A city in the sky. Constantine is dramatically split by a deep gorge, its old town
perched on a rock and linked by a series of spectacular bridges high above the
ravine. One of the most extraordinary city settings in the world.
*B-roll: suspension bridge over the gorge, cliff-top buildings, ravine below.*

### #8 — Santa Cruz Fort, Oran
Guarding the western coast. Above the lively port city of Oran, the Spanish-built
Santa Cruz Fort sits on a mountain beside a grand chapel, with panoramic views over
the Mediterranean. Oran itself is the birthplace of raï music.
*B-roll: hilltop fort, coastal panorama, Oran waterfront.*

### #7 — Tipaza
Roman ruins by the sea. The seaside archaeological park of Tipaza scatters ancient
Roman columns, basilicas and tombs right along the turquoise Mediterranean shore. A
UNESCO site where history and coastline meet beautifully.
*B-roll: seaside ruins, columns by the sea, turquoise water.*

### #6 — Timgad
The "Pompeii of Africa." Founded by Emperor Trajan, Timgad is a stunningly complete
Roman city laid out on a perfect grid in the highlands, with a triumphal arch,
theatre and paved streets still visible. One of the best-preserved Roman towns
anywhere.
*B-roll: Trajan's arch, grid streets, Roman theatre, columns.*

### #5 — Djémila
Roman grandeur in the mountains. Djémila — "the beautiful" — is a UNESCO Roman city
set among green hills, famous for its temples, forum and exquisite mosaics. Its
dramatic mountain setting makes it especially striking.
*B-roll: ruins among hills, temple, intricate mosaics.*

### #4 — Ghardaïa & the M'Zab Valley
A desert dreamscape of colour. The M'Zab Valley holds five ancient fortified
towns, their tightly packed houses tumbling in pink, white and ochre up the
hillsides around terracotta mosques. A UNESCO marvel of desert architecture.
*B-roll: hillside town, pyramidal minaret, colourful market.*

### #3 — The Martyrs' Memorial & Algiers
The proud capital. Algiers, "the White City," gleams in tiers above its bay, watched
over by the soaring concrete fronds of the Martyrs' Memorial — a monument to
Algeria's independence — with grand boulevards and white buildings below.
*B-roll: the memorial, white city tiers, bay of Algiers.*

### #2 — The Casbah of Algiers
A living maze of history. The UNESCO-listed Casbah tumbles down to the sea in a
labyrinth of steep alleys, Ottoman palaces, mosques and whitewashed houses. The
historic, atmospheric soul of Algiers.
*B-roll: steep white alleys, Ottoman doorways, rooftops to the sea.*

### #1 — Tassili n'Ajjer & the Sahara
And our number one is one of the wonders of the desert. Deep in the Sahara, the
Tassili n'Ajjer plateau is a UNESCO landscape of giant sandstone "rock forests,"
natural arches and thousands of prehistoric rock paintings up to 12,000 years old.
Vast, otherworldly and unforgettable. If you see one thing in Algeria, make it the
Sahara.
*B-roll: sandstone formations, rock art, dunes, desert stars.*
