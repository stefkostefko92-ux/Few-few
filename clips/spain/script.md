## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Spain — a country of sunshine, passion and breathtaking architecture. Moorish
palaces and Gothic cathedrals, Gaudí's wild masterpieces, tapas, flamenco and
golden beaches. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Spain. Let's
count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Sagrada Família, Alhambra, flamenco, red-and-yellow flags.*

### #10 — Plaza de España, Seville
We start with one of the most beautiful squares in Spain. Built for a 1929 expo,
Seville's Plaza de España is a sweeping half-circle of grand pavilions, bridges and
a canal, decorated in colourful ceramic tiles representing every Spanish province.
Pure Andalusian grandeur.
*B-roll: the curved plaza, tiled alcoves, rowing boats on the canal.*

### #9 — The Guggenheim, Bilbao
A building as famous as the art inside. Frank Gehry's Guggenheim Museum is a
swirling sculpture of titanium that transformed the city of Bilbao overnight.
Guarded by Jeff Koons' giant flower "Puppy," it's a landmark of modern
architecture in the Basque Country.
*B-roll: titanium curves, "Puppy" sculpture, riverside setting.*

### #8 — The Roman Aqueduct of Segovia
One of the best-preserved Roman monuments in the world. Segovia's aqueduct marches
across the city on 167 arches, built around 2,000 years ago — and entirely without
mortar. Towering 28 metres at its highest, it's an astonishing feat of engineering.
*B-roll: arches across the square, close-up stonework, old town behind.*

### #7 — La Concha Bay, San Sebastián
One of Europe's most beautiful city beaches. La Concha is a perfect golden
crescent curving around a sheltered bay in the elegant Basque resort of San
Sebastián — a city also famous as one of the world's great food destinations.
*B-roll: crescent beach from above, promenade, pintxos bars.*

### #6 — Park Güell, Barcelona
Gaudí's whimsical garden. Park Güell is a fairytale hilltop park of mosaic
lizards, undulating tiled benches and gingerbread-like buildings, all overlooking
Barcelona and the sea. A riot of colour and imagination.
*B-roll: mosaic salamander, serpentine bench, city view.*

### #5 — The Great Mosque–Cathedral of Córdoba
A breathtaking meeting of two worlds. The Mezquita of Córdoba is a forest of
red-and-white striped arches built as a great mosque over a thousand years ago —
with a Christian cathedral later raised in its very centre. Few buildings on Earth
are so beautiful or so layered with history.
*B-roll: endless striped arches, the cathedral nave within, courtyard.*

### #4 — Seville Cathedral & the Giralda
The largest Gothic cathedral in the world. Seville's cathedral holds the tomb of
Christopher Columbus beneath soaring stone vaults. Beside it rises the Giralda — a
former minaret turned bell tower — which you climb by ramp for sweeping views over
the city.
*B-roll: vast cathedral interior, Columbus tomb, view from the Giralda.*

### #3 — Plaza Mayor & the Royal Palace, Madrid
The grand heart of the capital. Madrid's Plaza Mayor is a stately arcaded square
that has hosted markets and festivals for centuries. Nearby stands the Royal
Palace, the largest in Western Europe by floor area, with over 3,000 lavish rooms.
*B-roll: arcaded plaza, palace façade, changing of the guard.*

### #2 — The Alhambra, Granada
One of the most beautiful palaces in the world. The Alhambra is a sprawling
Moorish fortress-palace crowning a hill above Granada, with intricate carved
stucco, tranquil courtyards, reflecting pools and the gardens of the Generalife —
all framed by the snow-capped Sierra Nevada. Magical.
*B-roll: Court of the Lions, carved arches, reflecting pools, hilltop view.*

### #1 — The Sagrada Família, Barcelona
And our number one is Gaudí's unfinished masterpiece. The Sagrada Família has been
rising over Barcelona for more than 140 years — a basilica unlike any other, with
soaring organic towers and an interior like a stone forest, glowing in coloured
light through its stained glass. Nearing completion at last, it's simply
breathtaking. If you see one thing in Spain, make it this.
*B-roll: exterior towers, the glowing forest-like nave, stained-glass colours.*
