## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Sweden — Scandinavian cool, through the play-offs and into the World Cup. A country
of beautiful old towns and royal palaces, endless archipelagos, an Arctic north of
reindeer and Northern Lights, and a hotel made entirely of ice. Here are the Top 10
landmarks you have to see in Sweden. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Stockholm old town, archipelago, Northern Lights, blue-yellow flags.*

### #10 — Gothenburg
We start on the west coast. Sweden's second city is a relaxed harbour town of
canals, leafy avenues and the fun Liseberg amusement park, and a gateway to a
stunning archipelago of rocky islands. Famous, too, for its seafood and café
culture — "fika."
*B-roll: canals, Liseberg, harbour islands, café scene.*

### #9 — Öland & the Kingdom of Crystal
Sun, windmills and glass. The long island of Öland, linked by a great bridge, is a
summer paradise of wooden windmills, beaches and the royal summer residence.
Nearby, the "Kingdom of Crystal" is the heart of Swedish glassmaking, where you can
watch master blowers at work.
*B-roll: windmills, island coast, glass-blowing.*

### #8 — The Göta Canal
A journey across the country. The historic Göta Canal winds through the Swedish
countryside, linking lakes and locks from coast to coast. Cruise it slowly past
meadows, castles and quiet villages — a relaxing slice of old Sweden.
*B-roll: canal boat, locks, countryside.*

### #7 — Visby, Gotland
A medieval Hanseatic gem. On the island of Gotland, the UNESCO town of Visby is
wrapped in a remarkable medieval stone wall, with cobbled lanes, church ruins and
rose-covered cottages. Step back centuries in this beautifully preserved town.
*B-roll: ring wall, cobbled lanes, church ruins, roses.*

### #6 — Drottningholm Palace
Sweden's royal Versailles. Just outside Stockholm, the UNESCO-listed Drottningholm
Palace is the private home of the royal family, set in grand baroque gardens with a
unique 18th-century court theatre and a Chinese pavilion. Elegant and serene.
*B-roll: palace and gardens, theatre, lakeside.*

### #5 — Kiruna & Swedish Lapland
The wild Arctic north. Kiruna is Sweden's northernmost city — in the land of the
midnight sun, the indigenous Sámi people and their reindeer, and gateway to vast
national parks. A frontier of pure wilderness.
*B-roll: snowy town, reindeer, Sámi culture, vast tundra.*

### #4 — Stockholm City Hall
Home of the Nobel banquet. The striking red-brick City Hall, with its tall tower
and golden hall shimmering with millions of gold-leaf mosaic tiles, hosts the
glittering Nobel Prize banquet each year. A masterpiece on the waterfront.
*B-roll: tower over the water, the Golden Hall, the Blue Hall.*

### #3 — The Icehotel, Jukkasjärvi
A hotel rebuilt from ice every year. In the Arctic village of Jukkasjärvi, the
famous Icehotel is carved fresh each winter from blocks of frozen river — with ice
beds, ice sculptures and an ice bar that melt away each spring. A magical,
once-in-a-lifetime stay.
*B-roll: ice rooms, ice sculptures, ice bar, snowy exterior.*

### #2 — Abisko & the Northern Lights
One of the best places on Earth to see the aurora. Thanks to a rare microclimate
that keeps its skies clear, Abisko National Park in the far north is famous for
glowing displays of the Northern Lights dancing over its frozen lake and mountains.
Unforgettable.
*B-roll: green aurora over mountains, frozen lake, the Aurora Sky Station.*

### #1 — Gamla Stan, Stockholm
And our number one is the soul of the capital. Gamla Stan is Stockholm's old town,
one of the best-preserved medieval city centres in Europe — a charming island maze
of narrow cobbled lanes, ochre and gold buildings, the Royal Palace and the grand
Stortorget square. Spread across fourteen islands, Stockholm is a stunner. If you
see one place in Sweden, make it this.
*B-roll: colourful old town facades, Royal Palace, narrow lanes, waterfront.*
