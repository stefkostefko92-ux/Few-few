## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Jordan — making history with their first-ever World Cup, and a country with some of
the greatest sights on Earth. A rose-red city carved into the rock, a Mars-like
desert, ancient Roman streets and the lowest point on the planet. Here are the Top
10 landmarks you have to see in Jordan. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Petra Treasury, Wadi Rum, Dead Sea float, black-white-green-red flags.*

### #10 — The Amman Citadel
We start in the capital. On a hill above Amman, the Citadel layers thousands of
years of history — Roman temple columns, a Byzantine church and an Umayyad palace —
with panoramic views over the white city and its Roman theatre below.
*B-roll: temple columns, hilltop ruins, city view, Roman theatre.*

### #9 — Madaba & Mount Nebo
The city of mosaics. Madaba is famous for its ancient mosaic map of the Holy Land on
a church floor. Nearby, Mount Nebo is the spot where Moses is said to have looked
out over the Promised Land — with sweeping views to the Dead Sea and beyond.
*B-roll: mosaic map, hilltop memorial, valley view.*

### #8 — The Dead Sea
Float on the lowest point on Earth. The Dead Sea lies over 400 metres below sea
level, its water so salty you bob effortlessly on the surface. Coat yourself in its
famous mineral mud and relax in one of the world's most surreal natural spas.
*B-roll: floating reader, salt formations, mineral mud, sunset.*

### #7 — Aqaba & the Red Sea
Jordan's seaside escape. On the Red Sea, Aqaba offers warm waters, vibrant coral
reefs and shipwrecks ideal for diving and snorkelling, plus a relaxed beach-town
vibe. The country's window on the sea.
*B-roll: coral reef, divers, beach, harbour.*

### #6 — The Dana Biosphere Reserve
Jordan's largest nature reserve. Dana sweeps from high mountains down to the desert
through dramatic canyons and rugged valleys, rich with wildlife and superb hiking,
including the trek toward Petra. Wild and beautiful.
*B-roll: layered canyons, mountain village, hiking trail.*

### #5 — Karak Castle
A mighty Crusader stronghold. Perched on a hill on the ancient King's Highway, Karak
is one of the largest Crusader castles in the region, a maze of stone halls,
passages and ramparts with commanding views over the valleys below.
*B-roll: hilltop castle, stone passages, ramparts, valley views.*

### #4 — Bethany Beyond the Jordan
A site of deep significance. On the banks of the Jordan River, this UNESCO site is
revered as the place where Jesus was baptised, with ancient churches and the gentle
river still drawing pilgrims today. Peaceful and historic.
*B-roll: river bank, ancient church ruins, pilgrims (respectful tone).*

### #3 — Jerash
One of the best-preserved Roman cities in the world. Jerash dazzles with colonnaded
streets, grand temples, two theatres and a vast oval plaza ringed by columns — so
complete you can almost hear the Roman crowds. Magnificent.
*B-roll: oval plaza of columns, colonnaded street, theatre, arch.*

### #2 — Wadi Rum
The Valley of the Moon. Wadi Rum is a breathtaking desert of red sand, towering
sandstone mountains and natural arches — so otherworldly it has stood in for Mars in
countless films. Ride a 4x4, sleep in a Bedouin camp and watch the stars blaze.
Unforgettable.
*B-roll: red desert, sandstone cliffs, 4x4, Bedouin camp, stars.*

### #1 — Petra
And our number one is one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. Petra is the
breathtaking "rose-red city," carved by the Nabataeans into towering sandstone
cliffs over 2,000 years ago. Walk through the narrow Siq canyon and the magnificent
Treasury suddenly appears before you — a moment you'll never forget. If you see one
thing in Jordan — or anywhere — make it this.
*B-roll: the Siq opening to the Treasury, carved façades, the Monastery, candlelight.*
