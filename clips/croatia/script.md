## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Croatia — the dazzling jewel of the Adriatic, and World Cup finalists who play far
above their size. Ancient walled cities, turquoise lakes and waterfalls, thousands
of islands and Roman ruins still in everyday use. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you
have to see in Croatia. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Dubrovnik walls, Plitvice falls, island beaches, red-white-blue flags.*

### #10 — Zagreb's Upper Town
We start in the capital. Zagreb's Gornji Grad — Upper Town — is a charming district
of cobbled streets, the colourful tiled roof of St Mark's Church, gas-lit lamps and
cosy cafés. The relaxed, cultured heart of Croatia.
*B-roll: St Mark's tiled roof, cobbled lanes, funicular, café terraces.*

### #9 — The Pula Arena
A Roman colosseum by the sea. In the Istrian city of Pula stands one of the
best-preserved Roman amphitheatres in the world, built 2,000 years ago to hold
20,000 spectators. Today it hosts concerts and film festivals beneath the stars.
*B-roll: amphitheatre arches, interior arena, concert lighting.*

### #8 — Zadar & the Sea Organ
Where the city sings. Zadar's seafront holds two modern marvels — the Sea Organ,
which plays haunting music as waves push air through pipes, and the Greeting to the
Sun, a glowing solar light show — set against what Alfred Hitchcock called the
world's most beautiful sunset.
*B-roll: waterfront steps, glowing light circle, sunset over the sea.*

### #7 — Krka National Park
Waterfalls you can admire up close. Krka is famous for its series of travertine
cascades tumbling through a green river canyon, with walkways winding right past
the thundering Skradinski Buk falls. Less crowded than Plitvice and utterly
beautiful.
*B-roll: wide cascading falls, wooden walkways, emerald water.*

### #6 — Rovinj
The most romantic town on the Istrian coast. Rovinj's pastel houses tumble down to
the sea, crowned by the tall tower of St Euphemia's church. Lose yourself in its
steep cobbled lanes and watch the sunset turn the harbour gold.
*B-roll: pastel waterfront, church tower, cobbled alleys, harbour sunset.*

### #5 — Hvar & the Dalmatian Islands
Island paradise. Croatia has over a thousand islands, and glamorous Hvar leads the
pack — lavender fields, a hilltop fortress and chic harbours, with the scattered
Pakleni islets just offshore. Sail the crystal-clear Adriatic and island-hop your
way along the coast.
*B-roll: harbour town, fortress view, sailing between islands, clear coves.*

### #4 — Split & Diocletian's Palace
A living Roman palace. The heart of Split is built inside the walls of a palace the
Roman emperor Diocletian raised 1,700 years ago — and people still live, eat and
shop among its ancient columns and courtyards. History you can walk straight
through.
*B-roll: palace cellars, the peristyle courtyard, bell tower, seafront Riva.*

### #3 — Plitvice Lakes
A natural wonderland. Plitvice is a string of sixteen terraced turquoise lakes,
linked by hundreds of waterfalls and connected by wooden boardwalks that let you
walk right over the crystal-clear water. A UNESCO masterpiece of colour and sound.
*B-roll: terraced lakes from above, boardwalks over the water, cascades.*

### #2 — The City Walls of Dubrovnik
A walk through history with a view. Dubrovnik's mighty medieval walls run almost
two kilometres around the old town, up to 25 metres high. Stroll the full circuit
for breathtaking views over the terracotta rooftops and the deep blue Adriatic
below.
*B-roll: walls above the rooftops, fortress towers, sea views.*

### #1 — The Old Town of Dubrovnik
And our number one is the "Pearl of the Adriatic." Within those walls, Dubrovnik's
old town is a gleaming maze of marble streets, baroque churches and the polished
Stradun promenade — so cinematic it became King's Landing on screen. By night,
lamplit and magical. If you see one place in Croatia, make it this.
*B-roll: the Stradun, marble streets, rooftops, cable-car view at sunset.*
