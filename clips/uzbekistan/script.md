## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Uzbekistan — making history with their first-ever World Cup, and the dazzling heart
of the ancient Silk Road. Turquoise-tiled domes and towering minarets, desert
fortress cities and ancient bazaars where East once met West. Here are the Top 10
landmarks you have to see in Uzbekistan. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Registan domes, blue tilework, desert city, blue-flag montage.*

### #10 — Tashkent
We start in the capital. Tashkent blends Soviet-era boulevards and modern towers
with leafy parks, bustling bazaars like Chorsu under its great green dome, and a
metro famous for its ornate, palace-like stations. The lively gateway to the
country.
*B-roll: Chorsu bazaar dome, ornate metro station, city squares.*

### #9 — The Chimgan Mountains
A green escape near the capital. The Chimgan range in the Tian Shan mountains offers
hiking, skiing and a scenic cable car, with alpine lakes and snow-dusted peaks — a
refreshing contrast to the desert cities.
*B-roll: mountain peaks, cable car, alpine lake.*

### #8 — Shah-i-Zinda, Samarkand
An avenue of tombs. This stunning street of mausoleums in Samarkand is lined with
some of the finest tilework in the Islamic world — a dazzling corridor of blue,
turquoise and gold mosaics. Breathtakingly beautiful.
*B-roll: tiled mausoleum corridor, intricate mosaics, domes.*

### #7 — The Aral Sea & Moynaq
A haunting reminder. Once one of the largest lakes on Earth, the Aral Sea has
largely vanished — and at Moynaq, rusting ships now sit stranded on what is now
desert. A sobering, unforgettable landscape and a lesson in environmental change.
*B-roll: ship graveyard in the desert, vast dry seabed.*

### #6 — Bibi-Khanym Mosque, Samarkand
A monument to ambition. Built by the conqueror Timur, the Bibi-Khanym Mosque was one
of the largest in the Islamic world, with a colossal entrance portal and grand blue
dome. Imposing and majestic.
*B-roll: huge portal, blue dome, courtyard.*

### #5 — Itchan Kala, Khiva
A complete walled desert city. The UNESCO old town of Khiva is a perfectly preserved
fortress city of mud walls, minarets, madrasas and palaces — including the famous
fat turquoise Kalta Minor minaret. Walking it feels like stepping into the Silk
Road past.
*B-roll: city walls, turquoise minaret, madrasas, rooftop view.*

### #4 — The Ark of Bukhara
A mighty royal fortress. The Ark is a massive ancient citadel that towered over
Bukhara for over a thousand years, home to the city's emirs. Its sloping walls and
gateway dominate the old town. Powerful and historic.
*B-roll: sloping fortress walls, grand gate, interior courts.*

### #3 — Po-i-Kalyan, Bukhara
The heart of holy Bukhara. This ensemble centres on the towering Kalyan Minaret —
so magnificent that Genghis Khan reportedly spared it — beside a grand mosque and a
beautifully tiled madrasa. The spiritual core of an ancient city.
*B-roll: tall minaret, tiled madrasa portal, mosque courtyard.*

### #2 — Gur-e-Amir, Samarkand
The tomb of a conqueror. The Gur-e-Amir mausoleum, with its fluted azure dome, is
the resting place of Timur (Tamerlane), one of history's great conquerors. Its
gilded interior is among the most beautiful in Central Asia.
*B-roll: ribbed blue dome, gilded interior, portal.*

### #1 — Registan Square, Samarkand
And our number one is the jewel of the Silk Road. The Registan is a breathtaking
public square framed by three towering madrasas, their vast portals and domes
covered in intricate blue and gold tilework. Lit up at night, it is one of the most
magnificent sights in the entire Islamic world. If you see one thing in Uzbekistan,
make it this.
*B-roll: the three madrasas, tiled portals, square at night, mosaics up close.*
