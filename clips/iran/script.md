## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Iran — ancient Persia, one of the great civilisations of human history. Dazzling
tiled mosques, the ruins of mighty empires, poets' gardens, desert cities and
legendary hospitality. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Iran. Let's
count them down.
*B-roll: montage — tiled domes, Persepolis, desert, green-white-red flags.*

### #10 — Golestan Palace, Tehran
We start in the capital. The UNESCO-listed Golestan Palace is a lavish royal complex
of mirrored halls, painted tilework and gardens — a glittering showcase of Persian
art and the seat of the Qajar dynasty. Opulent and beautiful.
*B-roll: mirrored hall, tiled façades, palace gardens.*

### #9 — The Tomb of Hafez, Shiraz
A shrine to poetry. Iranians cherish their poets, and the garden tomb of Hafez in
Shiraz is a place of pilgrimage, where people gather to read his verses beneath a
graceful pavilion among roses and cypress trees. Serene and deeply Persian.
*B-roll: garden pavilion, the tomb, roses, visitors reading.*

### #8 — The Old City of Yazd
A desert city of windcatchers. Yazd is one of the oldest continuously inhabited
cities on Earth, a UNESCO maze of earthen lanes, domes and tall "badgir"
windcatchers built to cool homes in the heat. A magical, atmospheric place.
*B-roll: earthen rooftops, windcatchers, alleys, rooftop sunset.*

### #7 — The Lut Desert & the Kaluts
One of the hottest places on the planet. The Dasht-e Lut is a vast UNESCO desert
where wind has carved enormous sand ridges called kaluts into a surreal city-like
landscape — spectacular at sunset and under blazing stars.
*B-roll: sculpted desert ridges, dunes, sunset, starry sky.*

### #6 — Nasir al-Mulk, the Pink Mosque
A kaleidoscope of light. In Shiraz, the Nasir al-Mulk mosque is famous for its
stained-glass windows, which on a sunny morning scatter rainbows of colour across
the carpeted floor and pink-tiled walls. One of the most photographed interiors in
the world.
*B-roll: coloured light on the floor, stained glass, pink tiles.*

### #5 — The Bridges of Isfahan
Where the city gathers. Isfahan's historic bridges — especially the elegant
multi-arched Si-o-se-pol and Khaju — span the river as both crossings and gathering
places, where locals sing beneath the arches in the evening. Beautiful and lively.
*B-roll: arched bridge lit at night, reflections, people gathering.*

### #4 — The Shrine of Imam Reza, Mashhad
A vast sacred complex. In Mashhad, the immense, golden-domed shrine of Imam Reza is
one of the holiest sites in Shia Islam and one of the largest mosque complexes in
the world, drawing millions of pilgrims to its dazzling courtyards each year.
*B-roll: golden dome, vast tiled courtyards, pilgrims (respectful tone).*

### #3 — Persepolis
The ceremonial capital of an empire. Near Shiraz stand the magnificent ruins of
Persepolis, built by Darius the Great 2,500 years ago — grand staircases, towering
columns and detailed carvings of subjects from across the ancient Persian Empire. A
UNESCO wonder.
*B-roll: grand staircase, columns, carved reliefs, gateway of nations.*

### #2 — Naqsh-e Jahan Square, Isfahan
One of the largest and most beautiful squares in the world. This UNESCO masterpiece
is framed by stunning blue-tiled mosques, a royal palace and the grand bazaar, all
around a vast space where families stroll and horse carriages pass. The glorious
heart of Isfahan.
*B-roll: aerial of the square, tiled portals, fountains, carriages.*

### #1 — The Imam Mosque, Isfahan
And our number one is the jewel of Persian architecture. Opening onto Naqsh-e Jahan
Square, the Imam Mosque dazzles with its towering blue-tiled portal, soaring dome
and intricate mosaics in every shade of turquoise and gold. Stand beneath the dome
and a whisper echoes around you. Simply breathtaking. If you see one thing in Iran,
make it this.
*B-roll: blue dome, tiled portal, mosaics, echoing interior.*
