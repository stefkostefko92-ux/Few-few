## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Tunisia — small, sunny and astonishingly rich in history. The ruins of mighty
Carthage, Roman colosseums, blue-and-white clifftop villages, island beaches and a
Sahara so cinematic it became another planet on screen. Here are the Top 10
landmarks you have to see in Tunisia. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — El Jem, blue Sidi Bou Said, Sahara, red-white flags.*

### #10 — The Bardo Museum, Tunis
We start with a treasure house. The Bardo holds one of the world's finest
collections of Roman mosaics, displayed in a former palace — vivid scenes of gods,
hunts and daily life lifted from across ancient Tunisia. A dazzling introduction.
*B-roll: grand mosaic halls, colourful Roman floors, palace interior.*

### #9 — Dougga
The best-preserved Roman town in North Africa. Spread across green hills, Dougga's
UNESCO ruins include a stunning Capitol temple, a theatre and streets you can still
walk. Less famous than Carthage, but wonderfully complete.
*B-roll: Capitol temple, hillside ruins, Roman theatre.*

### #8 — Kairouan
One of Islam's holiest cities. Kairouan's Great Mosque, with its vast courtyard and
ancient minaret, is one of the oldest and most important in the Muslim world. The
old medina around it is a UNESCO maze of whitewashed lanes and carpet workshops.
*B-roll: Great Mosque courtyard, minaret, medina lanes.*

### #7 — The Star Wars Sets & the Sahara
A galaxy far, far away — filmed here. The deserts around Tozeur and Matmata were
used as the planet Tatooine in Star Wars, and several sets still stand in the sand.
Add troglodyte homes carved underground and you have a truly otherworldly
landscape.
*B-roll: Star Wars set in the dunes, underground Berber homes, desert.*

### #6 — Tozeur & the Mountain Oases
Where the desert blooms. Tozeur is a gateway to vast palm groves and the dramatic
mountain oases of Chebika and Tamerza, with waterfalls and canyons hidden amid the
sand, beside the shimmering salt lake of Chott el Djerid.
*B-roll: palm oasis, mountain waterfall, white salt flats.*

### #5 — Djerba
The legendary island of the lotus-eaters. Sun-soaked Djerba offers golden beaches,
whitewashed villages, a colourful street-art quarter and one of the oldest
synagogues in Africa. Relaxed island life with deep history.
*B-roll: beaches, white domed houses, colourful murals.*

### #4 — The Medina of Tunis
A living medieval city. The UNESCO medina of the capital is a labyrinth of souks,
palaces and mosques — most famously the great Zitouna Mosque — packed with the
colour, scent and bustle of centuries of trade.
*B-roll: souk alleys, Zitouna Mosque, ornate doorways.*

### #3 — Sidi Bou Said
The blue-and-white jewel. Perched on a cliff above the Mediterranean, this
postcard-perfect village is a vision of cobalt-blue doors and shutters against
whitewashed walls, draped in bougainvillea. Pure Tunisian charm.
*B-roll: blue doors, clifftop café, sea views, bougainvillea.*

### #2 — Carthage
The ruins of a legendary empire. Once Rome's greatest rival, ancient Carthage spread
along the coast near Tunis. Today its scattered remains — the Antonine Baths, the
ports, hilltop ruins above the sea — recall a civilisation that nearly conquered the
Mediterranean.
*B-roll: seaside Roman baths, columns, hill above the bay.*

### #1 — The El Jem Amphitheatre
And our number one is one of the greatest Roman monuments in the world. Rising
straight out of a small town, the colosseum of El Jem once held 35,000 spectators
and is among the largest and best-preserved anywhere — so complete you can wander
its arena and underground passages. Breathtaking. If you see one thing in Tunisia,
make it this.
*B-roll: huge amphitheatre exterior, arena floor, underground chambers.*

villages of the south. That's the Top 10 landmarks of Tunisia. Which one would you
visit first? Subscribe — we're counting down every nation at the 2026 World Cup.
See you in the next country.
*B-roll: Sousse ribat, granary village, best-of montage, subscribe screen.*
