## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Senegal — the Lions of Teranga, African champions and a nation famous for its
warmth and "teranga," meaning hospitality. From a pink lake to colonial islands
and vast wildlife reserves, here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in
Senegal. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — Dakar coast, pink lake, colourful boats, green-yellow-red flags.*

### #10 — The African Renaissance Monument (Dakar)
We start with a giant. Towering above Dakar, this 49-metre bronze statue of a
family reaching toward the future is the tallest statue in Africa — taller than the
Statue of Liberty. Climb up for sweeping views over the capital and the Atlantic.
*B-roll: the towering statue, city and ocean view from the top.*

### #9 — Île de Gorée
A beautiful island with a heavy history. Just off Dakar, Gorée is a UNESCO site of
pastel colonial houses, bougainvillea and car-free lanes — and a profoundly moving
memorial to the transatlantic slave trade. Peaceful, poignant and unforgettable.
*B-roll: colourful houses, narrow lanes, harbour, ocean.*

### #8 — Lake Retba, the Pink Lake
A lake straight out of a dream. Lac Rose gets its astonishing bubblegum-pink colour
from salt-loving algae, especially vivid in the dry season. Workers wade in to
harvest salt by hand from its shores. Surreal and unique.
*B-roll: pink water, salt harvesters, mounds of salt on the shore.*

### #7 — Saint-Louis
Faded colonial elegance. Once the capital of French West Africa, the island city of
Saint-Louis is a UNESCO gem of crumbling pastel buildings, a famous iron bridge and
a lively fishing community. Atmospheric and full of character.
*B-roll: colonial facades, the Faidherbe bridge, fishing pirogues.*

### #6 — The Bandia Reserve
A safari close to the capital. Bandia is a wildlife reserve where you can spot
giraffes, rhinos, zebras, antelope and monkeys roaming among ancient baobab trees —
an easy taste of African wildlife not far from Dakar.
*B-roll: giraffes among baobabs, rhinos, safari vehicle.*

### #5 — The Great Mosque of Touba
The spiritual heart of Senegal. Touba's vast mosque, with its towering minarets, is
one of the largest in Africa and the centre of the Mouride brotherhood. Each year
it draws millions of pilgrims to one of the great gatherings in the Muslim world.
*B-roll: grand mosque, minarets, pilgrims (respectful tone).*

### #4 — The Djoudj Bird Sanctuary
A paradise for birds. One of the world's most important bird reserves, Djoudj on
the Senegal River hosts millions of migrating birds — great clouds of pelicans,
flamingos and herons. A UNESCO World Heritage natural wonder.
*B-roll: pelican colonies, flamingos, boat through wetlands.*

### #3 — The Sine-Saloum Delta
Where the river meets the sea. This UNESCO delta is a maze of mangrove channels,
islands, lagoons and shell mounds, rich with birds and traditional fishing
villages. Glide through it by pirogue for a serene, beautiful escape.
*B-roll: mangrove channels, pirogue, birds, fishing villages.*

### #2 — Dakar & Pointe des Almadies
The westernmost point of the African continent. Vibrant Dakar buzzes with markets,
music and colour, ending at the Pointe des Almadies, where the city meets the
crashing Atlantic. The energetic, creative capital of Senegal.
*B-roll: Dakar streets, markets, the rocky western point, surf.*

### #1 — The House of Slaves, Île de Gorée
And our number one is a place of deep meaning. On Gorée Island stands the House of
Slaves and its haunting "Door of No Return," through which captured Africans were
said to have left the continent forever. A solemn, powerful memorial visited by
leaders from around the world. Senegal's most significant landmark.
*B-roll: the House of Slaves, the Door of No Return, the ocean beyond (respectful).*
