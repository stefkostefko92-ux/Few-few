## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
In 2026, Mexico becomes the first country ever to host the World Cup three
times. Millions of fans will land here for the football — but the real magic of
Mexico is everything *around* the stadiums. Ancient pyramids, colonial cities,
turquoise sinkholes and Pacific cliffs. So before you pack your jersey, here are
the Top 10 landmarks you absolutely have to see in Mexico. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: fast montage — pyramid, beach, cathedral, fans waving Mexican flags.*

### #10 — Palacio de Bellas Artes (Mexico City)
We start in the heart of the capital with Mexico's grandest cultural landmark.
The Palacio de Bellas Artes is a dazzling blend of white Italian marble on the
outside and bold Art Deco inside, crowned by a shimmering glass curtain by
Tiffany depicting the Valley of Mexico. It's home to the Ballet Folklórico and
murals by giants like Diego Rivera. If Mexico City had a crown jewel of the arts,
this is it.
*B-roll: drone over the marble dome, interior murals, the Tiffany curtain.*

### #9 — Guanajuato
Next, a city that looks like it was poured into a valley and left to glow. The
colourful houses of Guanajuato stack up the hillsides in pinks, yellows and
blues, connected by twisting alleys and underground tunnels once built for the
silver-mining boom. Wander the Callejón del Beso — the "Alley of the Kiss" — so
narrow that couples on opposite balconies can lean in and touch. At sunset, the
whole city turns gold.
*B-roll: hillside panorama at dusk, narrow alleys, the funicular view.*

### #8 — Cabo San Lucas & El Arco
Down at the very tip of the Baja California peninsula, the desert meets the sea
in dramatic fashion. El Arco — "The Arch" — is a natural rock formation carved
by the ocean, marking the exact point where the Pacific meets the Sea of Cortez.
Sea lions lounge on the rocks, and in winter, grey and humpback whales cruise
right past. It's Mexico's most photographed natural landmark on the west coast.
*B-roll: boat approaching the arch, sea lions, whale breach.*

### #7 — Monte Albán (Oaxaca)
Time to go ancient. Perched on a flattened mountaintop overlooking Oaxaca, Monte
Albán was one of the earliest cities in the Americas, the capital of the Zapotec
civilisation for over a thousand years. Its vast central plaza, pyramids and
carved stone figures sit nearly 2,000 metres above sea level, with views in
every direction. Standing here, you feel just how advanced these cultures were
long before Europeans arrived.
*B-roll: aerial of the plateau plaza, carved stone "Danzantes", valley views.*

### #6 — Tulum
Now the postcard shot everyone knows. Tulum is the only major Maya city built
right on the coast, its weathered stone temples perched on a cliff above a
Caribbean sea so blue it almost looks fake. Below the ruins sits a perfect white
sand beach. Once a thriving port city, today it's where ancient history meets
turquoise paradise — arrive early to beat the crowds and the heat.
*B-roll: cliff-top temple, turquoise water, beach below the ruins.*

### #5 — The Cenotes of Yucatán
Hidden beneath the jungle of the Yucatán Peninsula is a secret world. Cenotes
are natural sinkholes — collapsed limestone caves filled with crystal-clear
freshwater, where shafts of sunlight pierce down into glowing blue pools. The
Maya considered them sacred gateways to the underworld. Today you can swim,
snorkel and dive in them. Ik Kil and Dos Ojos are among the most breathtaking on
Earth.
*B-roll: sunbeam into a cenote, swimmer in clear water, hanging vines.*

### #4 — Palenque
Deep in the rainforest of Chiapas lies the most atmospheric ruin in all of
Mexico. Palenque rises out of the jungle mist, its elegant Maya temples wrapped
in green and echoing with howler monkeys. The Temple of the Inscriptions hides
the tomb of the great ruler Pakal — one of the most important archaeological
discoveries in the Americas. Smaller than Chichén Itzá, but for many travellers,
far more magical.
*B-roll: temples in jungle mist, monkeys, the Temple of Inscriptions.*

### #3 — Teotihuacán
Just outside Mexico City stands a city so old that even the Aztecs found it
abandoned and called it "the place where the gods were created." Teotihuacán was
once one of the largest cities in the world, and its two colossal pyramids — the
Pyramid of the Sun and the Pyramid of the Moon — still dominate the landscape.
Walk the long Avenue of the Dead between them and try to imagine a metropolis of
a hundred thousand people. At sunrise, hot-air balloons drift over the whole
valley.
*B-roll: Pyramid of the Sun, Avenue of the Dead, hot-air balloons at dawn.*

### #2 — Zócalo & Metropolitan Cathedral (Mexico City)
Back in the capital for our number two. The Zócalo is one of the largest city
squares in the world, the beating heart of Mexico City — and of the entire
nation. On one side rises the Metropolitan Cathedral, the oldest and largest
cathedral in the Americas, built over three centuries on the ruins of the Aztec
capital Tenochtitlán. Right beside it, the Templo Mayor reminds you that this
exact spot has been the centre of power for 700 years.
*B-roll: aerial of the square with a giant flag, cathedral façade, Templo Mayor.*

### #1 — Chichén Itzá
And our number one is the icon of Mexico itself. Chichén Itzá, one of the New
Seven Wonders of the World, is crowned by El Castillo — the great pyramid of
Kukulcán. It's a giant stone calendar: 365 steps, one for every day of the year.
And twice a year, on the equinox, the setting sun casts a shadow down the
staircase that looks exactly like a serpent slithering to the ground — Maya
engineering so precise it still stuns scientists today. If you see one landmark
in Mexico, make it this one.
*B-roll: El Castillo wide shot, the equinox serpent shadow, ball court, crowds.*

sanctuaries of Michoacán, where millions of butterflies turn the forest orange.
