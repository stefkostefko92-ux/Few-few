## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
England — the very birthplace of football, and a country where history is around
every corner. Royal palaces and ancient stone circles, soaring cathedrals, rolling
green hills and one of the world's great cities. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you
have to see in England. Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — London skyline, Stonehenge, castles, England flags.*

### #10 — The Roman Baths, Bath
We start in one of England's most beautiful cities. In Bath, the Romans built a
spa around natural hot springs nearly 2,000 years ago, and the steaming Great Bath
still stands today. Surrounded by honey-coloured Georgian architecture, it's
history you can almost soak in.
*B-roll: the Great Bath steaming, Roman columns, Georgian crescents.*

### #9 — The Lake District
England's most beautiful landscape. The Lake District is a national park of
shimmering lakes, green fells and stone villages that inspired poets like
Wordsworth and Beatrix Potter. Hike the hills, cruise Lake Windermere, and breathe
in the country's wild, romantic heart.
*B-roll: lake and fells, stone cottages, hikers on a ridge.*

### #8 — Windsor Castle
The oldest and largest occupied castle in the world. For nearly a thousand years
Windsor has been a home of English and British monarchs. Tour the lavish State
Apartments, see St George's Chapel, and watch the Changing of the Guard at this
working royal residence just outside London.
*B-roll: castle ramparts, Changing of the Guard, St George's Chapel.*

### #7 — York Minster & the Shambles
Step into medieval England. The city of York is crowned by York Minster, one of
the largest Gothic cathedrals in Northern Europe, with breathtaking stained glass.
Nearby, the Shambles is a narrow medieval street of overhanging timber buildings
said to have inspired Harry Potter's Diagon Alley.
*B-roll: Minster towers, stained glass, the leaning Shambles street.*

### #6 — Hadrian's Wall
The northern frontier of the Roman Empire. Hadrian's Wall once stretched 117
kilometres across England, built to mark the edge of Roman Britain nearly 1,900
years ago. Walk its dramatic surviving stretches across windswept hills and stand
where Roman soldiers once guarded the empire's edge.
*B-roll: stone wall over rolling hills, ruined forts, hikers.*

### #5 — Stonehenge
One of the most famous prehistoric monuments on Earth. Standing on Salisbury Plain
for around 5,000 years, Stonehenge is a ring of colossal standing stones whose
purpose still puzzles experts. How — and why — ancient people raised these giant
stones remains one of history's great mysteries.
*B-roll: stone circle at sunrise, aerial of the henge, crowds at solstice.*

### #4 — Buckingham Palace
The London home of the British monarch. With its grand façade, golden gates and
famous balcony, Buckingham Palace is the ceremonial heart of the nation. Watch the
red-coated guards in their bearskin hats perform the Changing of the Guard — pure
royal spectacle.
*B-roll: palace façade, guards marching, the balcony, the Mall.*

### #3 — Tower of London & Tower Bridge
Nearly a thousand years of history on the Thames. The Tower of London has been a
fortress, palace and prison, and today guards the dazzling Crown Jewels, watched
over by the Beefeaters and its famous ravens. Beside it stands Tower Bridge, the
city's iconic Victorian drawbridge.
*B-roll: White Tower, Crown Jewels, Tower Bridge lifting.*

### #2 — The London Eye & the South Bank
A modern icon on the river. The London Eye is one of the world's tallest
observation wheels, lifting you high above the Thames for a sweeping view across
the whole city. Below, the buzzing South Bank links it to the heart of
Westminster across the water.
*B-roll: the wheel turning, city view from a capsule, South Bank crowds.*

### #1 — Big Ben & the Houses of Parliament
And our number one is the symbol of England itself. The Houses of Parliament line
the Thames in glorious neo-Gothic splendour, crowned by the world-famous clock
tower whose great bell, Big Ben, has chimed over London since 1859. There is no
more iconic sight in England. If you see one landmark, make it this.
*B-roll: Big Ben and Parliament from the river, Westminster Bridge, night lights.*
