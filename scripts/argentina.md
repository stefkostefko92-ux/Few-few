## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Argentina — the reigning World Champions and one of the most football-mad nations
on Earth. But beyond the stadiums lies a country of staggering range: subtropical
waterfalls in the north, glaciers and jagged peaks in the south, and a capital
that breathes tango. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Argentina.
Let's count them down.
*B-roll: montage — waterfalls, glacier, Buenos Aires streets, sky-blue flags.*

### #10 — Caminito, La Boca (Buenos Aires)
We start in the capital's most colourful corner. Caminito is a short, vibrant
street in the working-class La Boca neighbourhood, where houses are painted in
bold reds, blues and yellows and tango dancers perform on the cobblestones. It's
also the spiritual home of Boca Juniors and their legendary stadium, La Bombonera.
*B-roll: colourful houses, street tango, La Bombonera exterior.*

### #9 — Ushuaia, the End of the World (Tierra del Fuego)
At the very southern tip of the Americas sits the world's southernmost city.
Ushuaia is the gateway to Antarctica, wrapped in snow-capped mountains and facing
the Beagle Channel. From here you can sail past sea lions and penguins, or hike
into the wild Tierra del Fuego National Park. It feels like the edge of the world
— because it nearly is.
*B-roll: harbour with mountains, Beagle Channel boat, penguins.*

### #8 — Bariloche & the Lake District (Patagonia)
Often called the Switzerland of South America. Bariloche sits among alpine lakes
and forests, famous for its chocolate, its ski slopes and the jaw-dropping views
from the Circuito Chico drive. In summer the lakes shimmer deep blue; in winter
the peaks turn white. Pure Patagonian postcard.
*B-roll: lakeside town, panoramic drive, snowy peaks.*

### #7 — Quebrada de Humahuaca (Jujuy)
Far in the northwest lies a valley painted by nature itself. The Quebrada de
Humahuaca is a UNESCO-listed canyon where entire mountainsides glow in stripes of
red, orange, green and purple — most famously the Hill of Seven Colours at
Purmamarca. Add tiny Andean villages and you have one of Argentina's most surreal
landscapes.
*B-roll: Hill of Seven Colours, desert villages, layered rock.*

### #6 — Mendoza Wine Country
At the foot of the Andes spreads Argentina's wine capital. Mendoza's vineyards
produce the country's famous Malbec, with rows of vines running right up to
snow-capped peaks. Tour the wineries, then look up at Aconcagua — the highest
mountain in the Americas — towering nearby. Food, wine and mountains in one.
*B-roll: vineyards with Andes backdrop, wine tasting, Aconcagua.*

### #5 — Casa Rosada & Plaza de Mayo (Buenos Aires)
The political heart of the nation. The Casa Rosada — the "Pink House" — is the
presidential palace, famous for the balcony where Eva Perón once addressed the
crowds. It overlooks the Plaza de Mayo, the square where Argentine history has
played out for two centuries. Grand, dramatic and unmistakably Buenos Aires.
*B-roll: pink façade, the balcony, the plaza and cathedral.*

### #4 — Valdés Peninsula (Patagonia)
A wildlife spectacle on the Atlantic coast. The Valdés Peninsula is one of the
best places on Earth to see marine giants — southern right whales breach just
offshore, while sea lions, elephant seals and colonies of penguins line the
beaches. In the right season, orcas even hunt by beaching themselves on the sand.
*B-roll: whale breach, penguin colony, sea lions on the shore.*

### #3 — Mount Fitz Roy (El Chaltén)
A mountain so striking it became the outline on a famous clothing logo. Fitz Roy
rises in jagged granite spires above the town of El Chaltén, Argentina's trekking
capital. The hike to Laguna de los Tres rewards you with the peaks reflected in an
icy lake — one of the great views of Patagonia.
*B-roll: jagged spires at sunrise glowing red, trekkers, mountain lake.*

### #2 — Perito Moreno Glacier (Santa Cruz)
One of the few glaciers on Earth that is still advancing. Perito Moreno is a wall
of brilliant blue ice five kilometres wide and 70 metres tall, and you can stand
on viewing platforms right in front of it. Wait long enough and you'll hear a
crack like thunder as giant chunks calve into the lake below. Unforgettable.
*B-roll: blue ice wall, ice calving into the lake, walkways.*

### #1 — Iguazú Falls (Misiones)
And our number one is one of the natural wonders of the world. On the border with
Brazil, Iguazú is not one waterfall but nearly three hundred, spread across almost
three kilometres of jungle. The centrepiece — the Devil's Throat — is a roaring,
misty horseshoe so powerful it's hard to comprehend. Walkways take you right to
the edge. If you see one thing in Argentina, make it this.
*B-roll: Devil's Throat, panoramic falls, rainbows in the mist, walkways.*
