## Narration (voice-over script)

### Intro
Brazil — five-time World Champions and the very soul of football. But this giant
of South America is also a country of breathtaking nature and rhythm: golden
beaches, the Amazon, roaring waterfalls and a city watched over by an open-armed
statue. Here are the Top 10 landmarks you have to see in Brazil. Let's count them
down.
*B-roll: montage — Rio beaches, Amazon, Christ statue, yellow-and-green flags.*

### #10 — Pelourinho, Salvador (Bahia)
We start in Brazil's first capital. Pelourinho is the historic heart of Salvador,
a UNESCO site of colourful colonial mansions, baroque churches and steep
cobblestone streets. It's the centre of Afro-Brazilian culture — the birthplace
of capoeira, samba-reggae and some of the country's best street parties.
*B-roll: pastel colonial buildings, capoeira in the square, drumming.*

### #9 — São Paulo
The largest city in the Southern Hemisphere. São Paulo is a sprawling concrete
giant of skyscrapers, world-class food and nonstop energy. Ride to the top of the
Edifício Itália or Farol Santander for a skyline that stretches to the horizon in
every direction, then dive into its incredible restaurant and art scene.
*B-roll: endless skyline from above, Avenida Paulista, street art.*

### #8 — Lençóis Maranhenses (Maranhão)
One of the strangest landscapes on Earth. Lençóis Maranhenses looks like a desert
of rolling white sand dunes — but in the rainy season, thousands of crystal-clear
blue and green lagoons appear between them. Walk the ridges and swim in pools that
exist for only a few months a year. Truly surreal.
*B-roll: white dunes with blue lagoons from the air, swimmer in a lagoon.*

### #7 — Fernando de Noronha
A volcanic paradise in the Atlantic. This protected archipelago has some of the
most beautiful beaches in the world — think Baía do Sancho, regularly voted the
planet's best beach — with crystal water, dolphins and sea turtles. Visitor
numbers are limited to protect it, which keeps it pristine.
*B-roll: cliff-backed beach, dolphins, turtles, turquoise bays.*

### #6 — The Amazon Rainforest
The greatest rainforest on the planet, and most of it lies in Brazil. From the
river city of Manaus you can watch the "Meeting of the Waters," where two rivers
flow side by side without mixing, then venture into a jungle teeming with pink
river dolphins, monkeys, macaws and more species than anywhere on Earth.
*B-roll: aerial of river through endless green, Meeting of the Waters, wildlife.*

### #5 — Copacabana & Ipanema (Rio de Janeiro)
The most famous beaches in the world. Copacabana's curving sweep of sand and its
wave-patterned promenade buzz from morning football to midnight parties, while
neighbouring Ipanema offers golden sunsets framed by the Two Brothers mountains.
This is the beating, sun-soaked heart of Rio.
*B-roll: Copacabana promenade, beach football, Ipanema sunset.*

### #4 — The Pantanal
The world's largest tropical wetland and the best place in South America to spot
wildlife. The Pantanal's flooded plains are home to capybaras, caimans, giant
otters and — if you're lucky — jaguars prowling the riverbanks. For nature lovers,
it rivals any safari on Earth.
*B-roll: jaguar on a riverbank, capybaras, flooded plains at sunset.*

### #3 — Sugarloaf Mountain (Rio de Janeiro)
A granite peak rising straight out of the sea at the mouth of Guanabara Bay. Ride
the glass cable car in two stages to the summit of Sugarloaf for a 360-degree view
of Rio — beaches, mountains, the city and Christ the Redeemer in the distance.
Time it for sunset and the whole bay turns gold.
*B-roll: cable car over the bay, summit panorama, sunset over Rio.*

### #2 — Iguaçu Falls (Paraná)
On the border with Argentina, Iguaçu is one of the natural wonders of the world —
nearly three hundred waterfalls thundering across almost three kilometres of
jungle. The Brazilian side gives you the grand panoramic view, with walkways that
lead right into the spray of the Devil's Throat. Pure, overwhelming power.
*B-roll: panoramic falls, walkway into the spray, rainbows, coatis.*

### #1 — Christ the Redeemer (Rio de Janeiro)
And our number one is the icon of Brazil itself. Standing 30 metres tall with arms
outstretched atop Corcovado mountain, Christ the Redeemer watches over Rio de
Janeiro and is one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. The journey up through
the Tijuca forest by cog train, then the view over the whole city, is
unforgettable. If you see one thing in Brazil, make it this.
*B-roll: statue against the sky, aerial circling Corcovado, city far below.*
