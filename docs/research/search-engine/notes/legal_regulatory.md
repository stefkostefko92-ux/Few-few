## 1. US v. Google (search monopoly) — what remedies exist, who can be a "Qualified Competitor", and status through Oct 2026

### Takeaway
Judge Mehta's Final Judgment (entered 5 Dec 2025, effective 3 Feb 2026, six-year term) orders Google to share index *metadata* and user-side data and to offer 5-year search and search-text-ads syndication licences to "Qualified Competitors" — but the definitions are US-centric (competitor "in the United States", no US national-security risk, syndication only for US-origin human queries). As of mid-Oct 2026 nothing has been shared yet: the syndication template was filed 9–13 Oct 2026, the index/data template is due 27 Oct 2026, certification procedures for Qualified Competitors are not yet public, and the D.C. Circuit appeal is fully briefed.

### Cited Findings

**Liability and remedies chronology**
- Complaint filed 20 Oct 2020 (D.D.C. No. 1:20-cv-03010-APM; Colorado states' parallel case No. 20-cv-3715); trial findings of fact and conclusions of law on **5 Aug 2024** holding Google liable under Sherman Act §2 for unlawfully maintaining monopolies in "general search services and general search text advertising"; remedies findings entered **2 Sep 2025**; Final Judgment entered **5 Dec 2025** (ECF No. 1462). — [Final Judgment, ECF 1462](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf)
- **Effective date / duration:** "This Final Judgment will take effect sixty (60) days after the date on which it is entered … will expire six (6) years from the Effective Date" (§VIII); Technical Committee provisions (§VII.A) effective immediately on entry. — [Final Judgment §VIII](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf). The court confirmed "The Final Judgment became effective on February 3, 2026." — [Order denying partial stay, ECF 1514, 7 May 2026](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/google-mehta-denies-stay-pending-appeal-for-now-order.pdf). → Expiry ≈ 3 Feb 2032 unless extended/terminated early.
  - Conflict: a secondary source (ppc.land, via search summary) said remedies become effective "180 days after entry"; the judgment text says 60 days, and the court's own order confirms 3 Feb 2026. — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/google-files-appeal-challenging-six-year-search-remedies/)

**Exclusive-contract / distribution remedies (§III)**
- Google may not condition Play or other app licensing on distribution/preload/placement of Google Search, Chrome, Google Assistant or any Google GenAI product on devices sold in the US (§III.A–D); may not condition consideration or licences on OEMs/carriers refraining from distributing rival general search services, browsers or GenAI products (§III.E–G); no bundling of placement payments across access points (§III.H–J); any agreement paying for distribution/default placement must terminate within **one year** (§III.K); browser developers and Apple must be expressly permitted to promote rival search/GenAI products, and per-access-point defaults may not be tied (§III.L–M). Payments for non-exclusive default placement are not banned. — [Final Judgment §III](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf); [TopDisputes summary](https://topdisputes.com/disputes/us-v-google-search)

**Index data sharing (§IV.A)** — *metadata, not the full content index*
- Within 30 days of a Qualified Competitor's certification, Google must make available "at marginal cost": (1) for each document a unique DocID plus a notation of which documents Google considers duplicates; (2) a DocID→URL map; (3) for each DocID the time the URL was first seen, last crawled, its **spam score**, and a **device-type flag** — "for all websites in the full Web Search Index Google uses" for Google.com/Search app. "Nothing in Section IV is intended to transfer intellectual property rights of third parties to index users." — [Final Judgment §IV.A](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf)

**User-side data (§IV.B–C)**
- For the term of the judgment, at marginal cost and non-discriminatorily: user-side data used to build/operate the **GLUE** statistical model(s) and to train/operate the **RankEmbed** model(s); provided "at least twice", exact number/frequency to be set by the Court; no disclosure of algorithms, ranking signals or post-trained LLMs. Plaintiffs + Technical Committee (TC) set privacy/security safeguards first; Google then has up to 6 months to implement. Data is licensed for "the exclusive purpose of serving users through a General Search Engine, Search Text Ads, and/or a Third-Party GenAI Product"; no sharing/selling without TC/Court authorisation. — [Final Judgment §IV.B–C](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf)

**Search syndication (§V)**
- 5-year licence via real-time API: desktop + mobile ranked organic web results; user-facing query-rewriting (spelling correction/expansion); Local, Maps, Video, Images and Knowledge Panel features as provided under current syndication deals; financial terms "no worse than" any other syndication customer and no less favourable than the most favourable current terms; latency/reliability functionally equivalent. — [Final Judgment §V.A–B](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf)
- **Cap and taper:** use capped at **40% of the Qualified Competitor's annual U.S. queries** in year 1, declining over 5 years (pace set by Court with TC). Google may impose "ordinary commercial restrictions on scraping, indexing, or crawling the syndicated results". **Only queries "that originate in the United States, from human end users"**; synthetic queries and queries from a QC's own syndicators excluded. Google may not retain/use syndicated queries beyond the most limited current terms. — [Final Judgment §V.B.4, V.B.7, V.B.9–10](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf)

**Search text ads (§VI)**
- §VI.A: process for Google to report Search Text Ads auction changes to the TC/Plaintiffs. §VI.B: 5-year Search Text Ads Syndication Licence (all ad formats/extensions available in AdSense for Search-type products), QC included in Google's **Search Partner Network**, QC free to use other ad providers / no preferential placement for Google ads; only US-origin human queries. — [Final Judgment §VI](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf)

**Technical Committee (§VII.A)**
- Five members (experts in software engineering, IR, AI, economics, behavioural science, privacy/security); US, Colorado states and Google each pick one, those three pick two more; US appointee chairs. — [Final Judgment §VII.A](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf). First three appointed 21 Jan 2026; final two appointed **9 May 2026**; all five onboarded by 15 Jun 2026. — [Plaintiffs' Second Status Report, ECF 1552 (filed 17 Sep 2026)](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1552.0.pdf)

**Who is a "Qualified Competitor" (can a non-US company qualify?)**
- "Competitor" = "any provider of or potential entrant in the provision of (i) a General Search Engine (GSE) in the United States, (ii) Search Text Ads in the United States, or (iii) a GenAI Product in the United States." — [Final Judgment §IX](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf)
- §IX.V: "'Qualified Competitor' means a Competitor who meets the Plaintiffs' approved data security standards as recommended by the Technical Committee and agrees to regular data security and privacy audits by the Technical Committee; who makes a sufficient showing to the Plaintiffs, in consultation with the Technical Committee, of a plan to invest and compete in or with the GSE and/or Search Text Ads markets; and who does not pose a risk to the national security of the United States." Annual re-certification required. — [Final Judgment §IX.V](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1462.0.pdf)
- As of 17 Sep 2026: "Plaintiffs and the TC are currently working on establishing the procedures for certifying Qualified Competitors … determining the certification criteria, creating the application process … Once [done], information regarding the process and procedures will be made publicly available." — [ECF 1552 §III.D](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1552.0.pdf)

**Implementation status (district court)**
- 7 May 2026: Google's motion for a partial stay of the data-sharing and syndication provisions **denied without prejudice**; Plaintiffs must give Google and the Court **45 days' notice** before any certified QC can first access a data-sharing or syndication remedy, so Google can renew its stay request. Court noted Plaintiffs' "most optimistic" estimate of benefits reaching QCs "by late fall or early winter of this year". — [ECF 1514](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/google-mehta-denies-stay-pending-appeal-for-now-order.pdf)
- Syndication template: due 6 Oct → extended to 9 Oct 2026 (unopposed motion, ECF 1558); Plaintiffs' Joint Recommendation filed (ECF 1559/1560); redacted public **template "Qualified Competitor Syndication License Agreement"** filed 13 Oct 2026 (ECF 1561-1) with Google's redactions to financial terms; joint status report on Google's objections due 16 Oct 2026. — [ECF 1558](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1558.0.pdf); [ECF 1560](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1560.0.pdf); [ECF 1561](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1561.0.pdf)
- Template terms visible in redacted version: "Covered Query" = query "that originated from an End User in the United States"; agreement term **5 years**; ads paid as a **Revenue Share Percentage** of ad revenues (percentage redacted); governed by US federal common law / D.C. law with **exclusive jurisdiction of the D.D.C.** — [ECF 1561-1](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1561.1.pdf)
- Index + user-side data licence template: Plaintiffs to file by **27 Oct 2026**; joint status report on objections by **4 Nov 2026**. Google withheld some user-side-data information pending a Source Code Agreement; the TC says this delays work on privacy safeguards. — [ECF 1552 §III.C](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1552.0.pdf). Source Code Agreement template finalised per JSR of 25 Sep 2026. — [ECF 1555](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1555.0.pdf)
- TC indemnification dispute ongoing (JSR 11 Sep 2026; next report 2 Oct 2026). — [ECF 1548](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205/gov.uscourts.dcd.223205.1548.0.pdf)

**Appeal status (D.C. Circuit)**
- Google noticed appeal **16 Jan 2026** (ECF 1472); Plaintiffs cross-appealed **3 Feb 2026** (ECF 1484, 1485). — [ECF 1514](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/google-mehta-denies-stay-pending-appeal-for-now-order.pdf)
- D.C. Cir. Nos. **26-5023** (Google), **26-5047, 26-5049** (government cross-appeals seeking stronger remedies incl. a ban on default payments); Google's opening brief 22 May 2026 (asks to reverse liability and remedies); government brief 28 Jul 2026; Google reply **29 Sep 2026** closed briefing; on **5 Oct 2026** parties jointly asked for argument as early as **9 Dec 2026**; argument not yet scheduled per this source. — [TopDisputes (secondary)](https://topdisputes.com/disputes/us-v-google-search); [ppc.land on Google's brief](https://ppc.land/google-asks-appeals-court-to-throw-out-its-entire-search-monopoly-loss/)

### Inferences
- **A Bulgarian/EU company is not excluded on its face** (no nationality test), but it must be a provider or "potential entrant" in **US** general search / search text ads / GenAI, show a plan to invest and compete in those (US-defined) markets, pass US data-security standards and audits, and not be a US national-security risk. An EU-only, Bulgarian-language engine with no US plan would likely not qualify.
- The **syndication remedies are of little direct use for EU traffic**: they cover only US-origin human queries. The **index metadata** (DocID↔URL map, duplicate clusters, first-seen/last-crawled times, spam scores) is global and could seed a crawl frontier/spam filter — if a non-US entrant can get certified and the licence allows global use (license template not yet public; use is limited to serving users of a GSE/Search Text Ads/GenAI product).
- Earliest realistic access: after QC procedures are published, templates are approved (Oct–Nov 2026 filings), 45-day stay-notice runs, and any renewed stay motion is resolved — i.e., 2027 at the earliest, and contingent on the D.C. Circuit outcome (argument requested for Dec 2026).

### Gaps
- Exact text of the Sept 2, 2025 remedies memorandum opinion (ECF 1435/1436) was not read (Justia returned 403); only the Final Judgment and later filings were read.
- Whether the D.C. Circuit granted the 5 Oct 2026 request to expedite / set argument for 9 Dec 2026 — not confirmed (search quota exhausted; no primary docket access).

- Redacted financial terms of the syndication template (revenue-share %, fees) are not public.

## 2. EU Digital Markets Act (Reg. (EU) 2022/1925) — Google Search obligations: Art. 6(5), 6(3), 6(11)

### Takeaway
Google Search has been a designated core platform service since 6 Sep 2023 with full compliance due since 7 Mar 2024. On **16 Jul 2026** the Commission adopted a binding **Art. 6(11) specification decision** forcing Google to share anonymised ranking/query/click/view data with eligible search engines (incl. AI chatbots) from **January 2027** at cost-based FRAND prices; on **23 Jul 2026** it fined Google **€460m** for Art. 6(5) self-preferencing in Search. A brand-new Bulgarian entrant will not meet the Art. 6(11) eligibility thresholds on day one (2 years of EU operations or >€50m capital, plus ≥50,000 EU monthly users).

### Cited Findings

**Designation and legal texts**
- Commission designated Google Search, Play, Maps, YouTube, Android, Chrome, Shopping and online ads as core platform services on 6 Sep 2023 (decision of 5 Sep 2023); full compliance required since **7 Mar 2024**, including Art. 6(7) Android interoperability and Art. 6(11) access to anonymised search data. — [Commission press release IP/26/1634, 16 Jul 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1634); [IP/25/811, 19 Mar 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_25_811)
- Art. 6(11) DMA: gatekeeper must provide third-party online search engines, at their request, FRAND access to ranking, query, click and view data on free and paid search; personal data in query/click/view data must be anonymised. Art. 6(5): no more favourable ranking of own services; ranking must be transparent, fair, non-discriminatory. Art. 6(3): choice screen at first use of a gatekeeper's online search engine, virtual assistant or browser on its OS. — [DMA, Reg. (EU) 2022/1925 on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/1925/oj) (text not fetched this session; obligations as confirmed in Commission releases above and the [DMA data-access page](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/developer-portal/data-access/alphabet-specification-proceedings-sharing-google-search-data_en))

**Art. 6(5) self-preferencing — investigation → fine**
- Non-compliance investigation opened **25 Mar 2024**; preliminary findings **19 Mar 2025** (Search treats Google's shopping, hotel booking, transport, financial and sports results more favourably via top placement, dedicated spaces, enhanced visual formats and filters; Commission referenced "Articles 6(5) or 5(4)"). — [IP/25/811](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_25_811)
- **23 Jul 2026 non-compliance decision: €460m fine** (Search self-preferencing) + €430m (Play anti-steering) = €890m. Google ordered to "treat third-party services that feature on Google's search results in a fair and non-discriminatory manner"; **60 days** to comply or face periodic penalty payments up to 5% of worldwide turnover. Commission notes Google is testing changes for shopping/hotels/flights, and "takes note of Google's proposals on how it plans to apply the principles of the decision to **AI Overviews and AI Mode**, on which dialogue will continue." "Google may decide to appeal." — [IP/26/1670](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1670); [DMA site news, 23 Jul 2026](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/commission-fines-google-eur890-million-breaches-digital-markets-act-2026-07-23_en)

**Art. 6(11) search-data sharing — specification decision (case DMA.100209)**
- Specification proceedings opened **27 Jan 2026**; preliminary findings + proposed measures published for consultation **16 Apr 2026** (consultation to 1 May 2026); final decision **16 Jul 2026** under Art. 8(2) (statutory deadline 27 Jul 2026). Specification decisions are legally binding but carry no fine; separate non-compliance powers (fines up to 10% of turnover) remain. — [IP/26/1634](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1634); [Bratby Law analysis](https://bratby.law/dma-specification-decisions-google/); [DMA consultation page DMA.100209](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/dma100209-consultation-proposed-measures-google-search-data-sharing_en)
- Commission: the decision fixes aspects "which have made Google's data sharing offer ineffective so far"; AI chatbots offering search functionality are eligible; "subject to anonymisation, Google should share the same data that it collects to optimise its own search services"; multi-layered anonymisation developed "in line with the **draft** Joint Guidelines on the interplay of the DMA and GDPR by the Commission and the EDPB"; Google may assess before sharing whether a recipient poses "serious cyber security and data protection risks"; **Google must start sharing from January 2027**. — [IP/26/1634](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1634)
- **Data scope:** anonymised ranking, query, click and view data for free and paid search; queries from any access point, query metadata (language, device type), URLs viewed, user interactions, result position. Removed/altered: account info, search histories, precise timestamps, advanced filters, input format, ad URLs, invalid traffic; very long and rare-term queries suppressed; location generalised; dwell times bucketed. Algorithms not shared. — [DMA data-access page](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/developer-portal/data-access/alphabet-specification-proceedings-sharing-google-search-data_en)
- **Anonymisation:** k-anonymity — each user in a group of ≥**1,000** users sharing location, device type and query language (≈95% in groups ≥25,000). **Latency ≥7 days** after the query. Each beneficiary can access data for up to **5 years**. — [DMA data-access page](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/developer-portal/data-access/alphabet-specification-proceedings-sharing-google-search-data_en). Bratby adds contractual controls: 13-month retention cap, re-identification ban, ISAE 3000 assurance before access and annually. — [Bratby](https://bratby.law/dma-specification-decisions-google/)
- **Eligibility:** (a) offers an online search engine (AI chatbots with search qualify); (b) genuine economic activity in the EU for ≥2 consecutive years **or** credible new entrant <2 years old with "more than €50 million in capital investments"; (c) ≥**50,000 monthly average users** of its EU search services in the past year; (d) not subject to EU sanctions and not controlled by a third country posing serious cyber-security/data-protection risk; (e) data processed in the EEA or transferred under GDPR Chapter V; (f) independent audit before receipt, within 6 months of processing start, then at least annually. — [DMA data-access page](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/developer-portal/data-access/alphabet-specification-proceedings-sharing-google-search-data_en)
- **Pricing (FRAND):** incremental costs (preparation/formatting, storage for sharing, dissemination incl. onboarding/tools) + "a reasonable return on capital employed" capped at Alphabet's WACC; fixed (one-off) + variable (recurring) components; any exceptional extra margin may not exceed Google Search's operating margin and **does not apply to SMEs**. — [DMA data-access page](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/developer-portal/data-access/alphabet-specification-proceedings-sharing-google-search-data_en); pricing fixed for 5 years per [Bratby](https://bratby.law/dma-specification-decisions-google/)
- **Implementation timeline:** by end-Aug 2026 Google publishes eligibility application form + webpage on how to apply; by Sep 2026 template licence agreements, test samples (small real sample, synthetic set, larger representative set requiring auditor's reasonable-assurance report) and cost estimates; by Nov 2026 finalised anonymised dataset; by Jan 2027 finalised pricing offer. — [DMA data-access page](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/developer-portal/data-access/alphabet-specification-proceedings-sharing-google-search-data_en)
- Google's response: its chief legal officer said the decisions "risk undermining vital privacy and security guardrails for millions of Europeans"; no General Court challenge confirmed at time of Bratby's writing. — [Bratby](https://bratby.law/dma-specification-decisions-google/)
- Criticism (commentary, not law): ITIF and ICLE argue anonymisation strips the long-tail queries most valuable to rivals; ECIPE argues the measures may conflict with GDPR limits. — [ITIF comments, 1 May 2026](https://itif.org/publications/2026/05/01/comments-european-commission-proposed-measures-google-search-data-sharing/); [ICLE](https://laweconcenter.org/resources/icle-comments-to-the-european-commission-on-alphabets-article-611-dma-obligations/); [ECIPE](https://ecipe.org/insights/dma-proceedings-against-google/)

**Parallel Art. 6(7) decision (context)** — DMA.100220, same day: 11 Android features for third-party AI assistants; most due by Android 18 / 1 Aug 2027 at the latest; users benefit from July 2027. — [IP/26/1634](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1634); [Bratby](https://bratby.law/dma-specification-decisions-google/)

**UK context (non-EU)** — CMA designated Google with strategic market status in general search on 10 Oct 2025; conduct requirements on Fair Ranking and Data Portability (17 Jun 2026) but none yet on third-party search-data access. — [Bratby](https://bratby.law/dma-specification-decisions-google/)

### Inferences
- **Practical path for a Bulgarian startup:** launch, reach ≥50,000 average monthly EU users, operate ≥2 years, keep data in the EEA, pass an ISAE-3000-type audit → apply for Art. 6(11) data (as an SME, pure incremental-cost pricing). Alternatively raise >€50m capital to qualify as a new entrant.
- The 7-day latency and k≥1,000 anonymisation make the data useful for aggregate click/CTR models and query-popularity priors for **head/torso** queries (incl. Bulgarian-language queries if enough volume per location/device/language cell), not for freshness or long tail.
- The Art. 6(5) decision (and its extension to AI Overviews/AI Mode under dialogue) may improve visibility of vertical search services that a new engine could offer, but does not directly help a general engine.
- Choice screens (Art. 6(3)) are a distribution channel, but inclusion criteria are set in Google's compliance implementation (not researched here).

### Gaps
- Google's published Art. 6(11) application webpage/form (due end-Aug 2026), template licence and cost estimates (due Sep 2026) were not located (search quota exhausted).
- Whether Google has appealed the 16 Jul 2026 specification decision or the 23 Jul 2026 €460m fine to the General Court — not confirmed.
- Exact Art. 6(3) choice-screen eligibility criteria for including a new search engine on Android/Chrome in Bulgaria — not researched.
- Whether Microsoft Bing is subject to DMA obligations: believed **not** designated (Commission closed the Bing market investigation in Feb 2024), but this was not verified in this session.

## 3. Digital Services Act (Reg. (EU) 2022/2065) — duties of online search engines; VLOSE threshold; small engines

### Takeaway
A search engine is an "online search engine" intermediary service under the DSA. Below 45 million average monthly EU users it carries only the baseline intermediary-service duties (points of contact, legal representative if non-EU, T&Cs, transparency reporting — the latter waived for micro/small enterprises) plus a duty to publish user numbers every six months; the heavy systemic-risk regime (Arts. 34–43) applies only after Commission designation as a VLOSE (≥45M), four months after notification.

### Cited Findings
- **VLOSE threshold:** Section 5 obligations apply to "online platforms and online search engines which have a number of average monthly active recipients" in the Union "equal to or higher than 45 million" and which are designated under Art. 33(4); threshold adjustable by delegated act if EU population changes ≥5% (set at 10% of population); designation ends after a full year below threshold (Art. 33(5)); obligations start/stop **four months** after notification (Art. 33(6)). — [DSA Art. 33 (consolidated text mirror)](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_33.html); official text [EUR-Lex 2022/2065](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj)
- **User-number publication (applies to search engines of any size):** Art. 24(2): "providers shall publish for each online platform or online search engine" average monthly active recipients in the Union, by 17 Feb 2023 and **at least every six months**; Art. 24(3): providers of online platforms or of online search engines must communicate this to their Digital Services Coordinator (DSC) and the Commission on request. — [DSA Art. 24](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_24.html)
- **Micro/small exemption is worded for online platforms:** Art. 19(1): Section 3 (except Art. 24(3)) "shall not apply to providers of online platforms" that qualify as micro or small enterprises (Rec. 2003/361/EC). — [DSA Art. 19](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_19.html)
- **Transparency reports:** Art. 15(1) annual machine-readable content-moderation reports for all intermediary services; Art. 15(2): does not apply to providers "that qualify as micro or small enterprises … and which are not very large online platforms". — [DSA Art. 15](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_15.html)

### Inferences
- **Baseline for a small Bulgarian engine (all applicable since 17 Feb 2024):** single points of contact for authorities and users (Arts. 11–12), clear T&Cs incl. any content restrictions/algorithmic moderation (Art. 14), compliance with authority orders (Arts. 9–10), Art. 15 reports unless micro/small, and Art. 24(2) user-number publication every 6 months. Because Art. 19 exempts only "providers of online platforms", the safer reading is that a micro search engine *still* publishes Art. 24(2) numbers (it is cheap to do so). If the engine also hosts user content (reviews, comments, user-submitted links), it becomes a hosting service/online platform with notice-and-action (Art. 16) and statement-of-reasons (Art. 17) duties.
- Being a VLOSE (≥45M EU MAU, i.e. ~10% of EU population) is far beyond realistic reach for a new Bulgarian engine; but systemic-risk duties (risk assessments, audits, a non-profiling ranking option, ad repository, researcher data access, supervisory fee) would then apply.
- The DSC for a Bulgaria-established provider would be Bulgaria's designated coordinator (identity not verified here).

### Gaps

## 4. GDPR for search engines — right to be forgotten, territorial scope, delisting process, query-log retention, lawful basis, children

### Takeaway
A search engine is a **controller** for the personal data it indexes and displays (CJEU *Google Spain*, C-131/12, 13 May 2014), must handle delisting requests under GDPR Arts. 17/21 using the EDPB's Guidelines 5/2019 (final 7 Jul 2020; plain-language factsheet June 2026), delist across EU versions (C-507/17), treat sensitive-data links specially (C-136/17), and should keep identifiable query logs no longer than necessary — the Article 29 WP's long-standing benchmark is ≤6 months. Processing for search rests in principle on legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)).

### Cited Findings
- **C-131/12 Google Spain (13 May 2014):** by "searching automatically, constantly and systematically" a search engine "collects", retrieves, records, organises, stores and discloses data → "processing"; the operator is the **controller**; it "must ensure, within the framework of its responsibilities, powers and capabilities" compliance; a non-EU operator with an EU establishment selling ads is caught; the operator may be obliged to remove links from name-based results even if the publisher's page remains lawful. — [CJEU press release No 70/14](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2014-05/cp140070en.pdf)
- **EDPB Guidelines 5/2019 on the criteria of the right to be forgotten in search engine cases under the GDPR** — final version adopted **7 Jul 2020**; cites C-131/12 and **C-136/17 *GC and Others v CNIL*, judgment of 24 Sep 2019**; states that processing by search engine providers "is based, in principle, on the legitimate interest of the search engine provider". — [EDPB page](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-52019-on-the-criteria-of-the-right-to-be-forgotten-in-the-search_en); [Guidelines PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201905_rtbfsearchengines_afterpublicconsultation_en.pdf)
- **EDPB factsheet "Search engine delisting: when to act and what to do" (June 2026):** the right is not absolute — usable only if one of the **six grounds of Art. 17(1)** applies; **five exceptions** (Art. 17(3)) may justify refusal; under the **Art. 21 right to object the burden of proof is on the search engine** ("the presumption is in their favour" — result must be delisted unless the engine demonstrates compelling legitimate grounds); delisting removes the URL only from **name-based** results (content remains on the source and may appear for other queries); "As a general rule, you do not need to contact the webmaster"; **children's data**: "Data collected when the individual was a child is granted special protection … and usually warrants delisting" (example: forum post written at age 14); public-figure example: a corruption article about a senior official may be kept. — [EDPB factsheet, June 2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-06/edpb-factsheet-search-engine-delisting_en.pdf)
- **Query-log retention:** Article 29 Working Party Opinion 1/2008 on data protection issues related to search engines (WP148, adopted **4 Apr 2008**): "the Working Party does not see a basis for a retention period beyond 6 months"; providers retaining longer "will have to demonstrate comprehensively that it is strictly necessary". — [WP148 PDF](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2008/wp148_en.pdf)
- **C-507/17 Google v CNIL (24 Sep 2019)** — territorial scope of delisting (EU-wide versions plus measures to discourage EU users; no EU-law obligation of global delisting); **C-460/20 TU, RE v Google (8 Dec 2022)** — inaccuracy-based delisting requests, evidence burden, image thumbnails. — case pages [C-507/17](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-507/17) / [C-460/20](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-460/20) (CURIA blocked in this session; holdings stated from established case law and not re-fetched — verify).

### Inferences
- Operational must-haves from launch: a delisting web form (name-based delisting, EU-wide), a documented Art. 17/21 balancing workflow with the burden on the engine, special handling for minors' data and Art. 9/10 categories (health, criminal records), and audit logs of decisions — the Bulgarian DPA (КЗЛД) would be the lead authority for a Bulgaria-established engine.
- Privacy-by-design positioning: keep query logs non-identifiable, or ≤6 months if identifiable; avoid account-level profiling. This also matters for Art. 6(11) DMA eligibility (EEA processing, audits) and for any future US QC audits.
- Lawful basis: legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)) for indexing/serving; consent only for optional personalisation/cookies (ePrivacy rules apply separately).

## 5. Copyright & AI: DSM Directive TDM (Arts. 3/4) and press publishers' right (Art. 15); Bulgarian transposition (ЗАПСП); case law; EU AI Act obligations

### Takeaway
Bulgaria transposed the DSM Directive with amendments to the Copyright and Related Rights Act (ЗАПСП) published in **ДВ бр. 100 of 1 Dec 2023, in force 1 Dec 2023**. The commercial TDM exception (чл. 26е) allows reproduction for automated analysis by anyone with lawful access unless the rightholder opts out — and for online content the opt-out only counts if expressed "by technical means recognizable by the software" (a strict machine-readable test). The press publishers' right (чл. 90г–90ж) lasts 2 years and — unusually — defines "very short extracts" numerically: **title + first ≤100 characters + ≤128×128 px thumbnail + ≤3 s audio/video**; longer news snippets need a licence. German appellate case law (OLG Hamburg, *Kneschke v LAION*, 10 Dec 2025) likewise says natural-language opt-outs in T&Cs were not machine-readable. If the engine provides or fine-tunes a GPAI model, AI Act Art. 53 obligations (in application since 2 Aug 2025; Commission enforcement from 2 Aug 2026) apply; Art. 50 transparency duties apply from 2 Aug 2026.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Indexing = automated analysis/reproduction:** a crawler that copies pages to build an index relies on чл. 26е (Art. 4). In Bulgaria (and arguably EU-wide after OLG Hamburg), a **robots.txt disallow addressed to the crawler is the clearest "technical means recognizable by software"**; ignoring it would take the copying outside the exception. Natural-language T&C bans are weaker but courts diverge (Denmark vs. Hamburg) — treat them cautiously.
- **Snippets of Bulgarian news:** showing more than title + 100 characters (or larger thumbnails / >3 s clips) from a press publication <2 years old requires a licence from the publisher in Bulgaria; design SERP news snippets to fit the statutory "very short extract" box, or license. Hyperlinks and facts are free.
- **LLM answers:** generating summaries of news articles is not "very short extracts" — a licence or strict limitation would be needed for press content; the IETF aipref draft also treats summarisation as outside "search" (see §6).
- **AI Act role mapping:** using a third-party LLM to generate answers makes the operator a deployer/provider of an AI system → Art. 50 transparency (tell users they interact with AI; mark synthetic content) from 2 Aug 2026; training/fine-tuning and releasing its own GPAI model → Art. 53 obligations (copyright policy honouring opt-outs; public training-data summary). Search itself is not an Annex III high-risk use.

## 6. Crawling norms: robots.txt (RFC 9309), IETF AI preferences, RSL, Cloudflare default blocking — legal weight in the EU

### Takeaway
robots.txt (RFC 9309, 2022) is a voluntary protocol, but in the EU it acquires legal bite as the paradigmatic machine-readable TDM opt-out under DSM Art. 4(3) / ЗАПСП чл. 26е(4). The IETF AI-preferences vocabulary (which distinguishes `search` from `train-ai` and `ai-use`) is still an Internet-Draft without consensus (v-08, Sep 2026). RSL 1.0 and Cloudflare's July 2025 default-block of AI crawlers are private/market mechanisms with no statutory force, but they shape what a new crawler can reach in practice.

### Cited Findings
- **RFC 9309** "Robots Exclusion Protocol" (IETF, Sep 2022) — the standardised robots.txt format. — [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309) (not fetched this session; referenced by the IETF aipref charter).
- **IETF AIPREF WG:** chartered to standardise (1) a vocabulary for AI-related usage preferences, (2) attachment via well-known URIs, the Robots Exclusion Protocol and HTTP headers, (3) reconciliation of multiple expressions; out of scope: enforcement, crawler authentication, registries. Milestones: submit vocab and attachment drafts to IESG — target **Aug 2026**. — [IETF datatracker aipref](https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/aipref/about/)
- **draft-ietf-aipref-vocab-08** (14 Sep 2026; expires 18 Mar 2027): Proposed Standard intended; IESG state "I-D Exists"; text states its contents "DO NOT REFLECT CONSENSUS". Categories: `train-ai` (modifying a generative model's parameters), `ai-use` (asset used as input to a generative model when not supplied by the user), **`search`** (finding assets and directing users to their original location; must link to source; excerpts only to help judge relevance; **excludes generating summaries**; overrides train-ai/ai-use); values y/n, absent = unknown. — [draft-ietf-aipref-vocab](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-aipref-vocab/). Open Future (Dec 2025) described the WG as largely stalled since summer 2025. — [Open Future](https://openfuture.eu/blog/laion-round-2-machine-readable-but-still-not-actionable/)
- **RSL (Really Simple Licensing):** "an open content licensing standard for the AI-first Internet"; RSL 1.0 specification available; licensing models pay-per-crawl, pay-per-inference, attribution, subscription, free/Creative Commons; referenced from robots.txt; administered by the non-profit RSL Collective; supporters listed include Akamai, Cloudflare, Fastly, Creative Commons, O'Reilly, Reddit, Yahoo. — [rslstandard.org](https://rslstandard.org/)
- **Cloudflare "Content Independence Day" (1 Jul 2025):** default changed to "block AI crawlers unless they pay creators for their content"; pay-per-crawl marketplace announced; post does not specify treatment of classic search crawlers. — [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/content-independence-day-no-ai-crawl-without-compensation/)
- **Legal weight:** OLG Hamburg test — a reservation is machine-readable if an automated process can use it to block TDM; natural-language T&Cs failed (2021 facts). — [Open Future](https://openfuture.eu/blog/laion-round-2-machine-readable-but-still-not-actionable/). Bulgarian statute: opt-out for online content effective only via "технически средства, разпознаваеми от софтуера". — [ЗАПСП чл. 26е(4)](https://kttc.tu-sofia.bg/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/1_9_ЗАКОН-ЗА-АВТОРСКОТО-ПРАВО-С-ИЗМЕНЕНИЯТА-ОТ-2024.pdf)
- Third-party data sources contractually require respecting robots.txt: OWIL §3 cites robots.txt as a technical restriction users must respect. — [OWIL v1.0](https://openwebsearch.eu/owil-current/)

### Inferences
- Treat robots.txt as **legally binding in practice** for an EU crawler: honour `Disallow` for its own user-agent and `*`; identify the crawler with a stable user-agent + info URL; respect crawl-delay/rate limits.
- Expect the opt-out landscape to split "search" vs. "AI": many sites (Cloudflare default, publisher robots.txt) block AI-training bots but allow search bots — so **do not reuse a search crawler's fetches to train or ground generative models** without checking AI-specific signals; operate separate user-agents for search vs. AI features.
- RSL / pay-per-crawl create a possible paid path to content behind default blocks; legally these are contract terms, not copyright exceptions.

### Gaps
- Whether Cloudflare's default block distinguishes verified classic search crawlers (and how a new engine gets "verified bot" status) — not established from the fetched post.
- No EU court has yet ruled on the legal status of aipref/RSL signals (none found).

- **Google LLC v. SerpApi, LLC** (N.D. ### Inferences
- **Recommended licensing stack for an EU/BG MVP:** own crawler for Bulgarian/regional web (robots-compliant) + licensed fallback for the long tail from **Mojeek Business** (storage rights; EU infra; ads allowed) or **Staan** (EU jurisdiction, €2/1k) or **Brave** (if storage-rights terms are negotiated); Common Crawl for bootstrap discovery/link graph only, with own rights/GDPR assessment; OWI for R&D only until a commercial licence exists.
- **Avoid** building on Grounding with Bing for a search product: no raw results, no storage, Microsoft-only ads, and data leaves Azure's compliance boundary — incompatible with an independent, privacy-first EU engine.
- **Do not scrape Google/Bing SERPs**: contract (ToS) breach, potential EU database sui-generis-right issues, and in the US active DMCA anti-circumvention litigation; it would also undermine future DMA Art. 6(11)/US QC eligibility (audits, "genuine" independence).
- Costs at scale (illustrative arithmetic from list prices): 1M queries/month ≈ £2–3k (Mojeek), €2–4k (Staan), $5k (Brave Search).

## 8. EU and Bulgarian public funding / initiatives for European search independence

### Takeaway
The flagship Horizon Europe project **OpenWebSearch.eu** (grant 101070014) ended **28 Feb 2026**; work continues under the "European Open Web Search" initiative (coordinated largely by the Open Search Foundation, Passau leading tech) and spin-off projects (e.g., German **SOURCE**, €2.5m). NGI Zero Commons (NLnet) closed its final call on 1 Jun 2026, but NLnet's **Restack** fund remains open (next deadline **3 Nov 2026**, €5k–50k, FOSS-only). The most relevant capital for a Bulgarian company is the **EIC Accelerator** (grant <€2.5m + equity €1–10m; next 2026 full-proposal cut-off **4 Nov 2026**) plus Bulgarian ERDF programmes ПКИП and ПНИИДИТ (no open search-relevant call identified).

### Cited Findings
- **OpenWebSearch.eu:** Sep 2022 – **28 Feb 2026** (42 months); Horizon Europe grant No 101070014; 14 organisations from 7 countries; three open calls funded 9 technical, 2 infrastructure, 1 economic and 3 legal third-party projects; OWI prototype May 2025; first search API/front-end still in progress at project end; a 2024 market study estimated OWI ROI within four years and ~€4.5bn net benefit over a decade; continuation via the **European Open Web Search initiative**, "largely coordinated by the Open Search Foundation, with the University of Passau leading technical work", seeking financial/legal support from Brussels and Member States. — [OpenWebSearch.eu end-results post, 4 Apr 2026](https://openwebsearch.eu/title-42-months-in-the-making-openwebsearch-eu-project-end-results-look-promising/)
- **SOURCE** project (launched 1 Apr 2026): disinformation-detection database using OWI data; partners Passau, Kassel, LRZ, Open Search Foundation, Alliance4Europe; **€2,500,000 over three years** from Germany's BMFTR. — [OpenWebSearch.eu news, 22 Jul 2026](https://openwebsearch.eu/2026/07/)
- **Open Search Foundation** (German e.V.): working groups (Tech, Legal, Economy…); 2026 activities include #FreeWebSearchDay (11 Sep 2026) and a "Boosting Digital Sovereignty" conference (1 Oct 2026); companies can pursue "individual partnerships" (no commercial OWI licence described). — [opensearchfoundation.org](https://opensearchfoundation.org/)
- **NGI Zero Commons Fund (NLnet):** €5,000–50,000 grants (scalable); Horizon Europe grant No **101135429** (+ Swiss SERI); **thirteenth and final call closed 1 Jun 2026**; fund awards €21.6m through 2027. — [NLnet Commons Fund](https://nlnet.nl/commonsfund/)
- **NLnet open calls (Oct 2026):** **Restack** (open Internet stack, core infrastructure, "alternatives to closed online technologies with large userbases"; AI projects out of scope save one exception) and **CodeSupply** (pilot); deadlines on the 3rd of every odd month — next **3 Nov 2026**; €5k–50k; anyone may apply (individuals, companies, etc.); outputs must be FOSS/open access. — [NLnet funding](https://nlnet.nl/funding.html)

### Inferences
- Best-fit sequence for a small Bulgarian company: (1) NLnet Restack grant for open-source crawler/index components (FOSS condition — keep proprietary ranking separate); (2) participate in the European Open Web Search community to get early access to OWI data and influence the commercial licence; (3) EIC Accelerator once there is a TRL-6+ product and traction (the €50m-capital route to DMA Art. 6(11) data is unrealistic; the 2-year/50k-MAU route is the practical one); (4) watch ПКИП/ПНИИДИТ calls for innovation vouchers/digitalisation.
- Privacy-first, EU-jurisdiction positioning aligns with all EU programmes and with the DMA/GDPR analysis above.
